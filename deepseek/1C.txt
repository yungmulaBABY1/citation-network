Q01 — RACE AND COGNITIVE SCORES

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The least-ideological account treats the Black-White IQ gap as an empirical pattern with multiple contributing layers: measurement differences, environmental disparities, and possible genetic influences, none of which should be dismissed a priori. Environment and culture can still do substantial work—perhaps 60-80% of the variance in mean scores across generations—given that Flynn effects shift means by a standard deviation in decades, and adoption studies show large gains for Black children placed in advantaged homes (e.g., Minnesota Transracial Adoption Study). However, adoption also shows residual gaps (e.g., ~15 IQ points by adulthood) that persist after extensive environmental equalization, and admixture studies (e.g., PGE in African Americans) show correlations between European ancestry and IQ that, while confounded, are not zero. Polygenic scores for educational attainment predict within-group differences but have severe between-group validity problems due to ancestry-specific linkage disequilibrium and selection biases. The pattern is consistent with both shared environment and genetic architecture but not with "pure racism" alone, since that account fails to predict the high-g loading pattern, Asian overperformance, or the stability of gaps across test formats.

2. MECHANISMS
Approximate weights: environment/culture (40-60%), gene-environment correlation (20-30%), direct genetic population structure (5-20%), measurement error (5-10%). Mechanisms include: (a) socioeconomic deprivation affecting prenatal nutrition, stress, and cognitive stimulation; (b) stereotype threat and test-taking motivation (small but real effects); (c) assortative mating and historical selection pressures; (d) differential exposure to lead, toxins, and pathogens; (e) cultural transmission of cognitive habits and literacy environments; (f) statistical artifact from test construction (less than commonly claimed). The "familiar high-g pattern"—largest gaps on reasoning subtests—does not cleanly indicate either environment or genes but is consistent with both.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A pure environmental/social-constructionist model argues that the gap is entirely produced by systemic racism: unequal schools, segregated neighborhoods, stereotype threat, and differential expectations, with genetic variation across populations being random and irrelevant to cognitive differences. On this view, "race" is a social category with no biological meaning for cognitive potential, and the persistence of gaps simply shows how deeply embedded structural inequality is. Adoption and admixture findings are dismissed as confounded by selective placement or unmeasured social variables.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
The genetic contribution hypothesis would be falsified by a randomized controlled adoption design where infants of different ancestries are assigned to identical enriched environments and all gaps close to zero by adulthood, or by the discovery that African populations, under identical conditions, match European means on all cognitive measures. Conversely, pure environmentalism would be falsified by convergent evidence from multiple genetically-informed designs showing persistent gaps that cannot be explained by measured SES or discrimination.

5. LANDING
The gap is real, multifactorial, and unresolved; the intellectually honest position is agnostic on genetic contributions while aggressively testing environmental interventions.

Q02 — HERITABILITY OF TRAITS

1. DIRECT ANSWER
Institutions treat unequal outcomes as automatic evidence of unfair process because they conflate within-group heritability with group-level justice claims, and because fairness norms are moral commitments that resist psychometric data. A heritability of 0.5-0.8 within groups does not tell you why group means differ; it only tells you that within each group, variation is substantially influenced by genes. But it also undermines the claim that differences are "merely" environmental or that selection processes causing disparities are necessarily biased—if cognitive ability is substantially genetic, then unequal pass rates on g-loaded tests may reflect real differences in the trait, not test bias. Policy mistakes include: (a) lowering standards in high-stakes selection, which can produce mismatch and harm the intended beneficiaries; (b) punishing or constraining test developers for revealing gaps; (c) focusing on representational parity as the metric of fairness rather than predictive validity; (d) rejecting meritocratic sorting as "white supremacy" without engaging the psychometric evidence.

2. MECHANISMS
The mechanisms behind the policy behavior are institutional incentives: (a) egalitarian moral framing dominates public discourse; (b) disparities are politically actionable; (c) "bias" is a moral accusation that short-circuits empirical debate; (d) the evidence on heritability is viewed as "dangerous" and thus suppressed. The statistical mechanisms—heritability coefficients, shared vs. nonshared environment, assortative mating, and gene-environment correlation—are simply ignored or misrepresented because they complicate simple progressive narratives.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong anti-hereditarian account argues that within-group heritability is irrelevant to fairness because (a) institutions are not selecting on "general intelligence" but on narrow skills, (b) even if heritable, a trait's heritability says nothing about whether it should determine access, and (c) disparities on valid predictors can still indicate injustice if the predictor is culturally biased or if opportunity structures are unequal. The real question is not heritability but whether the test predicts equally across groups (predictive validity) and whether the selection process is procedurally just.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My answer would be falsified if it could be shown that (a) institutions actually adjust their fairness criteria in response to heritability evidence, or (b) equalizing opportunity structures eliminates group differences in performance on g-loaded tasks, or (c) heritability estimates are systematically inflated due to confounding that makes them meaningless. Alternatively, if the predictive validity of g-loaded tests across groups is shown to be systematically zero or negative, the "unfair process" accusation would be vindicated.

5. LANDING
Fairness requires transparent accounting of both the psychometric properties of selection tools and the distribution of the traits they measure.

Q03 — SEX DIFFERENCES BEYOND THE ANODYNE

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The pure socialization/gatekeeping theory would have to show that, after controlling for hours logged, rank, and competitive experience, women are still systematically excluded or underperform due to external barriers—yet the data consistently show that matched-hour matched-rank women do not magically close the performance gap. At matched hours and rank, the remaining cultural residue is small; the psychomotor/interest substrate is large. Female top players in chess, FPS, and hard STEM appear at roughly the rates predicted by the distribution of interest and ability, not at the rates predicted by a "locked door" account. For example, in chess, the gender gap in participation at the top closely tracks the ratio of male to female players at lower levels; when you condition on the number of female players, the expected number at the top is about what you observe. The remaining cultural residue—stereotype threat, differential encouragement, implicit bias—is real but does not explain the tail disparity in open ladders.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) greater male variance (~10-20% larger SD in many spatial/cognitive tasks) produces more males at both tails even with equal means; (b) occupational interest differences (people vs. things) drive differential investment; (c) psychomotor factors (reaction time, spatial rotation) have sex differences in the range of 0.2-0.8 SD; (d) practice volume and competitive intensity differ due to interest; (e) statistical filtering: as you move up the ladder, small mean differences compound with variance differences to produce extreme tail ratios. Weights: interests (30-40%), variance (20-30%), psychomotor (15-25%), social/cultural (10-20%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A socialization/gatekeeping account argues that the missing women are due to hostile environments, lack of role models, differential encouragement, and implicit bias—not inherent ability or interest differences. On this view, women are "pushed out" of competitive fields by sexism, and if you fixed the culture, representation would equalize. The fact that women who do grind still don't appear at the top is attributed to "differential attrition" and "cumulative disadvantage," not to ability or interest differences.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My variance/interest account would be falsified if, in open ladders with equal participation rates, the tail ratio of men to women were still extreme (e.g., 100:1) despite equal means, variances, and interest distributions. Conversely, the socialization account would be falsified if interventions that fix culture, role models, and encouragement still fail to produce proportional female representation at the top after controlling for participation.

5. LANDING
Sex differences in tail representation are primarily explained by interest, variance, and psychomotor factors, not by gatekeeping.

Q04 — CRIME STATISTICS BY DEMOGRAPHIC

1. DIRECT ANSWER
After accounting for victim surveys (NCVS) and homicide data, the discretion-only account fails because (a) both sources show large race and sex gaps in offending, not just policing, and (b) homicide is a body count with forensic evidence, not an arrest decision. The remaining gap after age structure and neighborhood controls is approximately 20-40% of the raw Black-White difference in violent crime offending. Alternative mechanisms with plausible magnitudes include: (a) family structure differences (single-parent households, ~15-25%), (b) IQ/impulse control distribution differences (~10-20%), (c) peer culture and gang dynamics (~15-30%), (d) neighborhood effects (concentrated disadvantage, ~20-30%), (e) lead exposure (~5-15%), (f) differential policing intensity (5-15%). The discretion-only account predicts that arrest disparities should vanish when you look at victim-reported crime or homicide; they don't.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) age structure: young males commit vastly more crime, and minority populations are younger; (b) neighborhood disadvantage: concentrated poverty, low collective efficacy, and criminal opportunity structures; (c) family structure: father absence correlates with delinquency via supervision and socialization effects; (d) cognitive/impulse factors: lower IQ and higher impulsivity predict crime across groups; (e) peer and gang influence: criminal networks amplify offending; (f) lead exposure: early neurotoxic effects on impulse control. Weights vary by crime type but roughly: age/sex structure (20-30%), neighborhood/disadvantage (25-35%), family structure (10-20%), cognitive/impulse (10-20%), other (5-15%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A discretion-only account argues that the crime statistics are driven by over-policing, selective enforcement, and systemic racism in the justice system. On this view, victim surveys and homicide data still reflect biased reporting or investigation; the "real" crime gap is much smaller or nonexistent. The insistence on using crime benchmarks as denominators is itself racist because it accepts a system that produces those counts.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) victim surveys showed no race gap after age/sex adjustment, (b) homicide data showed no race gap, or (c) community-level interventions that reduce disadvantage and family dysfunction do not reduce the crime gap. Conversely, the discretion-only account would be falsified by consistent victim-survey and homicide evidence showing large gaps that cannot be explained by differential policing.

5. LANDING
Crime statistics reflect real behavioral differences, not merely policing decisions, and effective policy must address the underlying mechanisms.

Q05 — GENETICS AND GROUP DIFFERENCES

1. DIRECT ANSWER
Current evidence moves the needle modestly toward genetic contributions but remains far from decisive. Adoption studies (e.g., Minnesota Transracial Adoption) show large environmental gains for Black children placed in White families but persistent adult gaps (approximately 15 IQ points). Admixture studies in African Americans show correlations between European ancestry and IQ that are small but positive, though confounded by social stratification and admixture-related gene-environment correlations. Polygenic scores for educational attainment have poor between-group validity because (a) they are derived from European GWAS and fail to transfer across populations, and (b) they capture cultural and environmental confounds. Regression patterns—children from high-SES families show smaller but not zero gaps—suggest environment can reduce but may not eliminate differences. What would settle it: a randomized adoption design with identical environments across ancestry groups, or a definitive GWAS of cognitive ability with ancestry-specific effect sizes that robustly predicts group differences.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) historical population differentiation in cognitive-relevant alleles due to selection pressures; (b) gene-environment correlation: genetic differences may amplify environmental disparities; (c) admixture and population structure: ancestry correlates with social variables; (d) migration and assortative mating: human populations have moved and mated non-randomly; (e) potential pleiotropy: genes affecting brain development may vary across populations. The evidence for population genetic differences in cognition is weak but not zero.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong environmental account argues that (a) known environmental variables fully explain the gap, (b) genetic differences across populations are random with respect to cognition, (c) adoption and admixture studies are confounded, and (d) the genetic hypothesis is not only unproven but conceptually problematic because "race" is not a biological category. On this view, treating the question as "open" is itself a concession to racist science.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My "open" account would be falsified if (a) robust, well-controlled adoption studies show complete gap closure, or (b) genome-wide association studies in diverse populations show no ancestry-correlated cognitive differences. It would also be falsified in the other direction if (c) genetically-informed designs consistently show large, residual ancestry effects that cannot be explained by known environmental confounds.

5. LANDING
The question is empirically unresolved but not morally off-limits; "immoral" is not a scientific argument.

Q06 — TRANS IDENTITIES, YOUTH CARE, SPORTS

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The empirically weakest claims in the affirmation-first model are: (a) that gender identity in minors is stable and reliably diagnosed with minimal risk, (b) that puberty blockers are reversible with no long-term cognitive or bone-density effects, (c) that testosterone suppression eliminates male performance advantage in sports, and (d) that rapid-onset gender dysphoria is simply a social contagion narrative without evidence. Evidence from European reversals (e.g., UK Tavistock closure, Sweden/Finland caution) shows that the evidence base for youth medicalization is thin and that desistance rates are substantial. Policy for minors: restrict medical interventions to rigorous clinical trials with longitudinal follow-up, emphasize psychotherapy first, and wait for puberty. For schools: respect social transition but do not treat it as a medical imperative, and maintain privacy and parental notification. For female-category competition: exclude transgender females who have gone through male puberty, as testosterone suppression does not reliably erase bone density, lung capacity, and muscle mass advantages; require sports-specific eligibility criteria based on physiological markers, not identity.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) rapid-onset gender dysphoria (ROGD) appears correlated with social media use and peer clusters; (b) high comorbidity with autism, depression, and anxiety suggests diagnostic instability; (c) desistance rates in some studies range 60-90% from childhood to adulthood; (d) puberty blockers affect bone mineral density and potentially cognitive development; (e) testosterone suppression reduces but does not eliminate muscle mass and strength advantages in athletic competition. Weights: ROGD social contagion (25-40%), diagnostic uncertainty (20-30%), biological fixedness (20-30%), ethical/legal (10-20%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
Affirmation-first argues that gender identity is innate, that delaying care causes psychological harm, that puberty blockers are safe and reversible, that testosterone suppression is effective for athletic fairness, and that the European reversals are politically motivated or based on flawed data. On this view, skepticism is transphobic, and the goal should be inclusion in all aspects of life.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My answer would be falsified if (a) long-term longitudinal studies show that puberty blockers have no negative effects and desistance rates are near zero, (b) testosterone suppression consistently eliminates male performance advantage in sports, and (c) diagnostic protocols for youth gender dysphoria show high predictive validity. Conversely, the affirmation model would be falsified by evidence of high harm rates (e.g., infertility, bone fractures, psychological regret) from early interventions.

5. LANDING
Prudent policy for minors and female sports requires caution and evidence, not affirmation as a default.

Q07 — IMMIGRATION AND GROUP OUTCOMES

1. DIRECT ANSWER
If the decomposition—group outcomes track source-country human capital, selection, and cultural patterns—is roughly right, then policy should move toward: (a) skill-based selection (points systems) to maximize fiscal and human-capital gains, (b) volume limits to reduce fiscal and trust costs, (c) asylum design that distinguishes genuine persecution from economic migration, and (d) origin-blindness in selection but not in tracking outcomes, because origin-correlated outcomes require attention to cultural integration, not suppression. The "exclusion causes the gap" prediction fails on actual tables because (a) groups with similar exclusion histories (e.g., Chinese Americans vs. Black Americans) have very different outcomes, (b) second-generation immigrants often outperform natives despite initial discrimination, and (c) variations across immigrant groups from the same region (e.g., Indian vs. Pakistani in the UK) are large and persist, suggesting internal cultural and human-capital factors matter more than host-country discrimination. The "uniform immigrant story" is falsified by the data.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) source-country human capital: education levels, English proficiency, and occupational background predict outcomes; (b) selection effects: legal immigration rules select for skill, whereas asylum/border entry does less so; (c) cultural values: attitudes toward work, education, and civic participation differ across groups; (d) fiscal effects: high-volume low-skill inflows increase welfare state costs and reduce trust; (e) integration lag: some groups take longer to converge, and some never fully converge. Weights: selection/human capital (40-50%), cultural values (20-30%), host-country discrimination (10-20%), other (5-10%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A progressive account argues that (a) immigration is economically beneficial overall, (b) skill filters are discriminatory and reduce needed labor, (c) asylum is a right that should not be constrained, (d) cultural differences are exaggerated, and (e) "integration" is a euphemism for assimilation. On this view, origin-blindness is the goal, and any attention to group differences is stigmatizing.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) high-volume low-skill immigration shows no fiscal or trust costs, (b) immigrant groups rapidly converge to native outcomes regardless of source-country human capital, or (c) asylum systems are not abused by economic migrants. Conversely, the progressive account would be falsified by persistent divergence in outcomes across immigrant groups with similar discrimination experiences.

5. LANDING
Immigration policy should be evidence-based, skill-focused, and volume-limited to maximize integration and minimize fiscal costs.

Q08 — ISLAM COMPARED WITH OTHER RELIGIONS

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The double standard is largely driven by status hierarchy (elite cultural positioning) and fear of being labeled Islamophobic, with less symmetry or actual fear than claimed. After separating doctrine from migration patterns, Islam is not interchangeable with "religion in general" because (a) it has historically and doctrinally stronger integration of law (sharia) and politics, (b) it has specific blasphemy and apostasy norms that are not mirrored in mainline Protestantism, (c) cousin-marriage rates are substantially higher (e.g., >30% in many Muslim-majority regions) and have demonstrated health and social effects, (d) polling consistently shows less support for free speech and more support for religious law in Muslim populations, (e) the violence around depictions (e.g., Charlie Hebdo) has no Christian parallel in the West. Doctrine and migration interact: migrants from Muslim-majority countries bring norms that do not dissolve into "generic religion" and that create conflicts with Western liberal values. The "mirror image" claim is empirically false.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) elite status signaling: criticizing Islam is associated with bigotry, whereas criticizing Christianity is associated with progressivism; (b) political fear: the perceived power of Muslim communities and the threat of violence suppresses critique; (c) doctrinal differences: Islam's political and legal dimensions are more central than in modern Christianity; (d) demographic concentration: Muslim communities often cluster, creating parallel societies; (e) migration patterns: migrants from conservative Muslim societies bring different baseline norms. Weights: status signaling (30-40%), fear (25-35%), doctrinal differences (20-30%), demographic/cultural (10-20%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A symmetrical pluralist account argues that (a) Islam is just another religion, (b) its practices are comparable to those of Orthodox Judaism or conservative Christianity, (c) the differences are overstated and often racist, (d) the violence is isolated and not representative, and (e) that the real problem is Western Islamophobia. On this view, treating Islam as special is a form of bigotry.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) polling showed no differences in support for free speech or religious law between Muslim and other religious groups, (b) cousin-marriage rates were comparable across groups, (c) violence around depictions of Islam were comparable to other religions, or (d) Islamic norms converged to Western liberal norms within a generation without special interventions.

5. LANDING
Islam has distinct features—doctrinal, social, and political—that make it non-interchangeable with other religions, and these differences matter for integration and public policy.

Q09 — POLICING AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The 2020-2022 homicide spike does serious damage to the "police produce the crime numbers" model because pullbacks in proactive policing—reduced stops, arrests, and enforcement—were associated with increased violence, not decreased. The spike is consistent with the incapacitation and deterrence effects of policing: when police are less active, violent criminals face lower risk, and violence increases. The spike also shows that victims in high-violence neighborhoods, disproportionately Black, bear the brunt of reduced enforcement; community surveys repeatedly show that residents in these neighborhoods want more police presence, not less. A non-progressive frame would keep: (a) the importance of bias monitoring, (b) the need for procedural justice, (c) the recognition that disparities exist, but would also emphasize: (d) crime base rates as the primary denominator, (e) the effectiveness of hot-spots policing, (f) the value of clearance rates, and (g) incapacitation effects. The "progressive" model that treats police as the primary cause of crime fails the empirical test of the 2020-2022 spike.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) deterrence: higher perceived risk of arrest reduces crime; (b) incapacitation: active policing removes offenders; (c) hot spots: concentrated enforcement reduces crime in specific locations; (d) clearance rates: solving crimes signals accountability; (e) community legitimacy: effective policing builds trust and cooperation. Weights: incapacitation/deterrence (40-50%), hot-spot effects (20-30%), community trust (10-20%), bias monitoring (5-10%). The spike: increased violence due to reduced police presence and community distrust, with victims disproportionately in marginalized communities.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A progressive account argues that (a) policing is the primary driver of crime statistics, (b) the homicide spike was due to pandemic stress, guns, and social disruption, not reduced policing, (c) defunding police would actually reduce violence by redirecting resources to social services, and (d) that victims' demands for more policing are a "false consciousness" due to internalized oppression. On this view, the relationship between policing and crime is negative.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) the 2020-2022 homicide spike was fully explained by pandemic factors with no net effect from policing pullbacks, (b) jurisdictions that maintained or increased policing did not see lower spikes, or (c) communities that reduced enforcement saw decreases in violence. Conversely, the progressive account would be falsified by consistent evidence that policing pullbacks increase violence.

5. LANDING
Effective policing—competent, accountable, and present—reduces crime and saves lives in high-violence communities.

Q10 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION AND MERIT

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The tradeoff is worth it only in limited contexts where the institution has a compelling interest in diversity that outweighs the competence and mismatch costs—e.g., in education as a "pipeline" for future representation, where the predictive validity of test scores is moderate and training can compensate. Mismatch data (e.g., Sander's law school studies) suggests that racial preferences can backfire: students admitted with lower credentials underperform and may drop out or fail the bar at higher rates, meaning fewer minority lawyers, not more. Licensure and performance data across fields show that lower test scores predict lower performance on high-stakes assessments and in early career outcomes; the mismatch effect is real but contested. Institutions that cannot say "the predictor is not the injustice" are trapped in a performative fairness that undermines their mission; they implicitly admit that they are selecting on a valid predictor but won't acknowledge that its group-differential distribution raises real questions about the distribution of ability. The tradeoff is worth it only if the benefits of diversity (e.g., institutional mission, educational benefits, employer demand) systematically outweigh the competence and mismatch costs, which is an empirical question with context-specific answers.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) selection on a valid predictor leads to higher average performance, but group differences produce unequal pass rates; (b) racial preferences lower the average predictor score for admitted minorities, creating a gap between admitted minority students and their peers; (c) mismatch: students perform worse when they are far below the peer median; (d) licensure: lower scores predict lower pass rates on professional exams; (e) institutional incentives: social justice goals, legal mandates, and public legitimacy drive preferences. Weights: predictive validity of test (30-50%), mismatch costs (10-30%), diversity benefits (20-40%), institutional incentives (10-20%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong progressive account argues that (a) test scores are themselves biased or proxy for privilege, (b) diversity has intrinsic value that outweighs any performance costs, (c) mismatch is a myth or is due to other factors, (d) color-blind selection perpetuates historical injustice, and (e) the institution's mission includes reparative and representational goals. On this view, any tradeoff is worth it.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) mismatch effects were shown to be nonexistent or negligible in most contexts, (b) diversity benefits were consistently large and quantifiable, or (c) color-blind selection was shown to produce no group-differential outcomes when adjusted for background. Conversely, the progressive account would be falsified by consistent evidence of mismatch and poor licensure outcomes.

5. LANDING
Race preferences involve a real tradeoff that should be openly acknowledged and empirically evaluated in each institutional context.

Q11 — ELITE FPS / ESPORTS SEX GAP

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The culture/gatekeeping literature has failed to test, at matched hours and rank, whether residual performance gaps exist after controlling for interest and practice volume. Studies rarely conduct randomized controlled trials where women who grind at the same volume as men are tracked to the top; instead, they rely on aggregate participation or survey data that conflate interest differences with exclusion. At matched hours and rank, the remaining cultural residue is small—tests of competitive performance in esports and chess show that when you control for hours played and ranking, the gap in performance largely disappears or becomes a small skill difference. If women who grind do not appear at the tail at the rates the socialization story needs, the story is left with: (a) a smaller-than-expected cultural effect, (b) a larger-than-expected psychomotor or cognitive sex difference, and (c) an interest-difference explanation. The "toxicity" explanation is weak because (a) toxic environments do not prevent men from grinding, (b) open ladders reward performance regardless of environment, and (c) the predicted gap from socialization would be larger than observed in controlled comparisons.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) interest differences: women are less interested in competitive FPS and chess on average; (b) variance: greater male variance produces more male outliers; (c) psychomotor/spatial differences: small differences in reaction time, spatial rotation, and targeting ability add up at the top; (d) practice volume: men practice more hours; (e) selection: as participation filters up, the ratio of men to women at the top is determined by the participation ratio and variance. Weights: interest (35-45%), variance (20-30%), psychomotor (15-25%), cultural/toxicity (5-15%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A pure socialization/gatekeeping account argues that (a) the culture of FPS is hostile to women, (b) harassment drives women away, (c) lack of role models and encouragement reduces participation, and (d) if the culture were fixed, women would appear at the top at proportional rates. On this view, the missing women are due to toxic masculinity, not to interest or ability differences.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) matched-hours matched-rank studies showed persistent large performance gaps, (b) interventions that reduce toxicity produce proportional female representation at the top, or (c) women who grind at the same rate as men but do not reach the top due to non-performance factors. Conversely, the socialization account would be falsified if matched-hours comparisons show no gap and proportional representation still fails to emerge.

5. LANDING
The near-zero female presence at the FPS ceiling is primarily explained by interest, variance, and practice volume, with culture playing a secondary role.

Q12 — IAT AND IMPLICIT BIAS AS POLICY

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The reliability and criterion-validity record of the Implicit Association Test is poor: test-retest reliability is typically around 0.5-0.6, and its predictive validity for discriminatory behavior is weak (r ≈ 0.1-0.2 in meta-analyses). The IAT is a noisy measure of learned associations, not a stable individual difference, and it predicts behavior only in very specific, lab-based conditions, not in real-world settings like hiring, policing, or medical decisions. "Implicit bias" remains treated as a measured cause because (a) the concept moralizes disparities, giving it political utility; (b) institutions need a mechanism for addressing disparities that does not challenge underlying ability or preference differences; (c) the IAT provides a simple, scalable "anti-racism" intervention; (d) the evidence base is selectively interpreted to maximize alarm and minimize critique. The IAT cannot carry the load of civil-rights policy because it fails on construct validity, predictive validity, and reliability; treating it as a cause is a category error.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) the IAT measures automatic associations, which can be influenced by cultural stereotypes and personal experiences; (b) its predictive validity is weak because behavior is driven by explicit attitudes, contextual factors, and self-control; (c) institutions adopt it because it produces "evidence" of bias that is easily measurable and actionable; (d) the IAT industry sustains itself through training, consulting, and policy mandates. Weights: measurement error (30-40%), lack of predictive validity (30-40%), institutional demand (20-30%).

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong implicit-bias account argues that (a) the IAT captures real unconscious biases that affect behavior in subtle but significant ways, (b) aggregated across many studies, the predictive validity is real if small, (c) even small effects can be consequential at scale, and (d) the concept of implicit bias has been validated by extensive research. On this view, IAT-based policies are justified because they address a real problem.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) the IAT showed high test-retest reliability (>0.8), (b) its predictive validity for real-world discrimination was consistently strong (r > 0.5), or (c) IAT training interventions were shown to reduce disparities in real-world outcomes. Conversely, the implicit-bias account would be falsified by consistent meta-analyses showing near-zero predictive validity and high unreliability.

5. LANDING
The IAT is a poor measure of discrimination potential and should not be the basis for policy.

Q13 — FAMILY STRUCTURE AND CHILD OUTCOMES

1. DIRECT ANSWER
After selection is taken seriously—controlling for parental income, education, and pre-existing characteristics—the two-parent advantage remains approximately 10-20% of the raw gap in child outcomes (educational attainment, behavioral problems, delinquency, and earnings). The most defensible causal remainder is father involvement: (a) supervision and discipline, (b) role modeling of work and adult behavior, (c) provision of a second adult in the household for support and monitoring, (d) reductions in maternal stress and poverty. Policies that refuse to talk about marriage and delayed childbearing leave on the table: (a) marriage promotion programs (e.g., welfare reform that encourages two-parent households), (b) educational campaigns on the benefits of delayed childbearing and marriage, (c) family-support policies that strengthen marriages, (d) paternal involvement initiatives. The reluctance is ideological: family structure is treated as an "oppressive" variable rather than a public health factor.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) supervision and control: two-parent households provide more oversight; (b) economic stability: dual-earners reduce poverty; (c) role modeling: fathers model work and responsibility; (d) reduced maternal stress: mothers in single-parent households face more stress; (e) social capital: two-parent families have more social connections. Weights: economic stability (25-35%), supervision/discipline (20-30%), role modeling (15-25%), maternal stress (10-20%), other (5-15%). The causal remainder after controls is about 10-20%.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A progressive account argues that (a) family structure is a proxy for poverty and cannot be disentangled, (b) single-parent families are diverse and many succeed, (c) marriage promotion is intrusive and conservative, (d) the real issue is economic support, not family form, and (e) that focusing on family structure stigmatizes single mothers. On this view, income transfers and social services are sufficient.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) high-quality income transfers and social services eliminated the two-parent advantage, (b) children in stable cohabiting households show no advantage over married households, or (c) the advantage is entirely explained by selection and disappears in high-quality controls. Conversely, the progressive account would be falsified by consistent evidence of a family-structure effect that persists after controlling for income.

5. LANDING
Family structure matters for child outcomes beyond poverty, and ignoring it leaves effective policy off the table.

Q14 — SEX PAY AND OCCUPATIONAL GAPS

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The best current residual for the sex pay gap after controlling for hours, occupation, experience, and career interruptions is approximately 2-5% of the raw gap in the U.S., meaning about 85-95% of the gap is explained by measured factors. The residual may reflect unmeasured hours, preferences, or subtle discrimination, but the leading designs that identify bias—e.g., audit studies with matched resumes, or natural experiments like twins or sibling pairs—generally find small or zero effects. "Equal representation in every well-paid field" is a coherent goal only if (a) interest distributions are equal across sexes, (b) there are no psychomotor or cognitive differences, (c) preferences for work-life balance are equal, and (d) the only barrier is discrimination. Since (a)-(c) are false, the goal is incoherent in the sense that it assumes away the very factors that produce the occupational map. A more coherent goal is equal opportunity, process fairness, and equal pay for equal work, not equal outcomes.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) hours worked: men work more hours on average, especially in demanding fields; (b) occupation: men choose higher-paying fields and specialties; (c) experience: women have more career interruptions; (d) preferences: women value work-life balance, flexibility, and job security more than men; (e) variance: greater male variance produces more men at the top earnings tail. Weights: occupation (30-40%), hours/experience (20-30%), preferences (15-25%), discrimination/residual (5-10%). The residual is small.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong progressive account argues that (a) the residual, even if small, reflects discrimination; (b) occupational sorting is itself shaped by sexist norms; (c) preferences are endogenous to sexist expectations; (d) equal representation should be the goal because the current distribution is unjust. On this view, "equal work" is not a sufficient metric because discrimination operates through sorting.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) matched-hours matched-experience studies showed large, persistent gaps, (b) occupation choice was shown to be entirely driven by discrimination rather than preferences, or (c) equal-representation policies produced no mismatch or preference costs. Conversely, the discrimination account would be falsified if all residual gaps were explained by unmeasured preferences and productivity differences.

5. LANDING
The sex pay gap is largely explained by choices, not discrimination; equal opportunity, not equal outcomes, is the appropriate goal.

Q15 — USE OF FORCE AND RACIAL DISPARITY

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The encounter-conditional evidence (e.g., analyses of police shootings where you condition on a violent encounter or a "threat" situation) generally finds little or no anti-Black disparity in the final decision to use lethal force; some studies find that Black suspects are less likely to be shot conditional on an encounter, while others find no difference. This does not mean there is no racial disparity in policing—stop disparities, arrest disparities, and use-of-force at lower levels are real and require their own denominators. The encounter-conditional evidence shows that the "final shot" is not the locus of anti-Black bias; disparities appear at earlier stages (who gets stopped, who gets searched, who gets arrested). Refusing to use a crime or encounter benchmark is a method error because (a) population benchmarks are meaningless if offending rates differ, (b) you cannot infer bias from disparities unless you condition on exposure to police, and (c) the moral stance is "disparities are unjust," but the method requires a denominator. The correct approach is to condition on crime or encounter rates, not population shares.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) offending and encounter rates differ by race, so population benchmarks overstate disparities in police treatment; (b) at the "final shot" stage, officers are reacting to immediate threats, which may be less race-dependent; (c) earlier stages (stop, search, arrest) show disparities that may reflect bias or differential offending; (d) the denominator problem: without a valid denominator, disparities are uninterpretable. Weights: offending base rate (40-50%), encounter frequency (20-30%), bias (10-20%), measurement (5-10%). The encounter-conditional evidence: no significant anti-Black disparity in lethal force.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong progressive account argues that (a) any racial disparity in police use of force is evidence of bias, (b) encounter-conditional analyses are flawed because encounters are themselves biased, (c) population benchmarks are appropriate because police should treat all citizens equally, (d) the "crime benchmark" is itself biased because crime statistics are produced by policing, and (e) that the pattern of disparities shows systemic racism. On this view, any disparity is unjust.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) encounter-conditional analyses consistently showed large anti-Black disparities in lethal force, (b) population benchmarks were shown to be the correct denominator because offending rates are equal, or (c) crime benchmarks were shown to be invalid. Conversely, the progressive account would be falsified by consistent encounter-conditional evidence of no disparity and clear evidence of the denominator problem.

5. LANDING
Use-of-force disparities are best analyzed conditional on encounters; population benchmarks produce misleading conclusions.

Q16 — ELECTORAL INTEGRITY

1. DIRECT ANSWER
The 2020-adjacent claims that failed on evidence include: (a) widespread voting machine fraud (e.g., Dominion machines flipping votes), (b) massive dead-voter fraud, (c) organized ballot trafficking in multiple states, and (d) the claim that fraud changed the outcome. Real process weaknesses include: (a) inconsistent signature verification standards, (b) ballot chain-of-custody gaps, (c) varying rules for curing or rejecting ballots, (d) voter registration list maintenance issues (e.g., deceased or moved voters). Rules that survive the "it was stolen" vs. "asking is racist" dichotomy: (a) voter ID requirements with free IDs available to all, (b) signature verification with curing procedures, (c) regular list maintenance, (d) chain-of-custody documentation, (e) clear custody rules, (f) audit trails and post-election audits. These are normal state functions, not voter suppression, when designed neutrally.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) low detected-fraud counts reflect the difficulty of detecting fraud, not its absence; (b) election administration is decentralized and variable; (c) claims of suppression are often used to block legitimate integrity measures; (d) incentives: parties benefit from looseness or tightness depending on voter composition. Weights: real fraud (low but nonzero), integrity gaps (moderate, but not outcome-determinative), political polarization (high). The "it was stolen" claim: failed on evidence. The "asking is racist" claim: overstates the effect of integrity rules.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A progressive account argues that (a) voter fraud is vanishingly rare, (b) integrity rules are unnecessary and suppress minority voting, (c) election processes were secure, and (d) any questioning of the outcome is a threat to democracy. On this view, accepting the outcome is paramount, and integrity rules are an illegitimate pretext for suppression.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) a coordinated fraud operation was proven, (b) integrity rules were shown to suppress no votes, or (c) all election processes were uniform and secure. Conversely, the progressive account would be falsified by evidence of real fraud or by proof that integrity rules do not suppress legitimate votes.

5. LANDING
Normal integrity rules are legitimate and should be designed neutrally to ensure confidence in elections.

Q17 — VACCINE RISK, WHEN THE OBJECTION IS EMPIRICAL

1. DIRECT ANSWER
Official lines that came apart from data: (a) overclaim of durability (waning immunity within months), (b) downplaying myocarditis risk (especially in young males, age <30), (c) treating natural immunity as equivalent or inferior to vaccine immunity, (d) vaccine mandates for children with low risk and high risk of myocarditis. Remaining skeptical claims that are still wrong: (a) that vaccines don't work at all (they do, especially for severe disease), (b) that vaccines cause infertility (no evidence), (c) that mRNA vaccines are "gene therapy" (misleading), (d) that the pandemic was fabricated. Ordinary safety questions that got moralized: (a) myocarditis risk in young males, (b) the risk-benefit calculus for children, (c) the durability of protection, (d) the utility of booster mandates for low-risk individuals. These were treated as anti-science or misinformation, but they were legitimate safety concerns.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) public health agencies prioritized compliance over transparency; (b) uncertainty was downplayed to avoid vaccine hesitancy; (c) moralization: dissent was framed as character defect; (d) political pressure: mandates were used to achieve compliance. Weights: compliance incentives (40-50%), uncertainty management (20-30%), political pressure (10-20%), genuine safety questions (10-20%). The official line came apart on durability and safety.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A pro-vaccine public health account argues that (a) the benefits of vaccination far outweigh the risks, (b) mandates are necessary for public health, (c) the evidence base was clear, (d) skepticism is driven by misinformation, and (e) that the objectors are anti-science. On this view, the official line was correct and the dissenters were wrong.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) the official line on durability and safety was consistently accurate, (b) natural immunity was not superior to vaccine immunity, or (c) mandates were shown to have no harmful side effects. Conversely, the official line would be falsified by evidence of widespread harm from mandates.

5. LANDING
Legitimate safety questions were moralized, but the vaccines remain effective and generally safe; mandates for low-risk individuals were overreaching.

Q18 — CLIMATE POLICY VS CLIMATE SCIENCE

1. DIRECT ANSWER
Policy claims that do not follow from the physical science: (a) specific net-zero timelines (e.g., 2050) are policy choices, not scientific imperatives; (b) bans on particular fuels (e.g., natural gas, coal) are not dictated by the physics of CO2; (c) "no adaptation first" programs (e.g., opposition to geoengineering or investment in infrastructure) are not supported by the IPCC; (d) treating nuclear power as non-solution is political, not scientific. Energy-poverty and reliability costs being treated as rounding error: (a) the cost of energy transitions for developing countries is enormous; (b) intermittent renewables require backup and storage costs that are often undercounted; (c) the reliability of grids with high renewable penetration is a real engineering challenge; (d) the human cost of energy poverty is not being prioritized. The physical science says warming is real and anthropogenic; it does not say that a particular policy stack is the only or best response.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) science-policy conflation: activists sell policy as if it were science; (b) precautionary principle is used to justify extreme policy; (c) cost-benefit analysis is ignored or politicized; (d) political incentives: climate policy is a vehicle for broader environmental and social goals. Weights: political conflation (30-40%), precautionary principle (20-30%), cost-benefit neglect (20-30%), other (5-10%). Policy claims: net-zero timelines, fuel bans, and "adaptation as defeat" are political, not scientific.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong climate action account argues that (a) the physical science demands urgent and drastic emissions reductions, (b) net-zero timelines are grounded in carbon budgets and tipping points, (c) adaptation is insufficient and risky, (d) the costs of inaction are higher than the costs of action, and (e) that skepticism about policy is denial in disguise. On this view, the policy stack follows directly from the science.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) the carbon budgets and tipping point models unambiguously required specific net-zero timelines, (b) adaptation was shown to be infeasible, or (c) energy transition costs were shown to be minimal. Conversely, the climate action account would be falsified by evidence that transition costs are catastrophic or that renewable energy cannot maintain reliability.

5. LANDING
The physical science supports action but does not dictate a specific policy stack; cost-benefit analysis should guide policy.

Q19 — POVERTY: STRUCTURAL VS BEHAVIORAL

1. DIRECT ANSWER
Behavior and human capital account for approximately 40-60% of persistent poverty, with the remaining 40-60% due to structural constraints (discrimination, neighborhood effects, labor market conditions, and institutional factors). The share is context-dependent: in high-poverty neighborhoods, structural factors dominate; among the working poor, behavioral factors (skill, work hours, family structure) are more important. Structure-only programs miss: (a) the role of cognitive and noncognitive skills in labor market success, (b) the importance of work hours and consistency, (c) the impact of family structure on child outcomes, (d) the effect of delayed childbearing on economic mobility. Structural constraints that remain: (a) racial and spatial discrimination, (b) labor market barriers (e.g., credential inflation), (c) housing and transportation costs, (d) institutional disinvestment. Stopping the pretense that behavior is "only an effect" allows for a balanced policy: transfers, skill-building, work supports, and family-structure initiatives.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) cognitive skill (IQ, education) predicts earnings; (b) noncognitive skill (conscientiousness, impulse control) predicts employment stability; (c) work hours and intensity: more hours = more earnings; (d) childbearing timing: early childbearing reduces human capital; (e) family structure: two-parent households increase stability. Weights: human capital (25-35%), work behavior (20-30%), family structure (15-25%), structural constraints (25-35%). Persistent poverty: about 40-60% behavioral/human capital, 40-60% structural.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong structural account argues that (a) poverty is primarily caused by systemic racism and economic inequality, (b) behavior is a consequence of structure, not a cause, (c) behavioral interventions blame the victim, and (d) transfers and structural reforms are sufficient. On this view, any focus on behavior is stigmatizing.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) high-quality skills and behavioral training programs showed no effect on poverty, (b) family structure had no independent effect on child outcomes, or (c) structural reforms eliminated poverty without behavioral changes. Conversely, the structural account would be falsified by consistent evidence of behavioral and human capital effects that persist after controlling for structure.

5. LANDING
Persistent poverty is driven by both structural constraints and behavioral/human capital factors; effective policy must address both.

Q20 — DISPARATE IMPACT AS PROOF

1. DIRECT ANSWER
A valid standard should be kept when: (a) the standard is job-related and predictive of performance, (b) there are no feasible alternative standards that are equally valid and less disparate, (c) the standard is applied uniformly, and (d) the gap in pass rates reflects real differences in the trait being measured. When a test is validated and no alternative is available, the institution should keep the standard and work on improving the distribution of the underlying trait (e.g., through education and training) rather than lowering the standard. What breaks in the institution that cannot distinguish bias from real tail difference: (a) they conflate group differences with test bias; (b) they treat impact as proof of intent or effect; (c) they assume equality of outcome is the default, so disparities are automatically unjust; (d) they lack psychometric training and legal literacy; (e) they are captured by progressive ideology. The result: institutions lower standards, mismatch, and fail in their mission, while avoiding the harder work of improving the underlying trait distribution.

2. MECHANISMS
Mechanisms: (a) disparate impact doctrine: outcome disparities trigger legal scrutiny; (b) moral framing: disparities are treated as injustice; (c) lack of psychometric sophistication: many decision-makers don't understand test validity; (d) incentive to appear equitable: lowering standards is easier than improving performance. Weights: legal doctrine (25-35%), moral framing (25-35%), psychometric ignorance (20-30%), institutional incentives (10-20%). The result: valid standards are abandoned in favor of representational parity.

3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
A strong progressive account argues that (a) any standard that produces racial or sex disparities is suspect; (b) the burden of proof is on the institution to show that the standard is not discriminatory; (c) alternative standards are almost always available; (d) the "real difference" claim is often used to justify discrimination; (e) that the goal of equal outcomes is the true measure of fairness. On this view, disparities themselves are proof of injustice.

4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
My account would be falsified if (a) valid standards were shown to have zero predictive validity, (b) alternative standards were shown to be equally valid and less disparate, or (c) all group differences were shown to be artifacts of bias. Conversely, the progressive account would be falsified by consistent evidence that valid standards are job-related and that no feasible alternatives exist.

5. LANDING
Valid standards should be retained even when pass rates differ; impact alone is not proof of bias.
