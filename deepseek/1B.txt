Q01 — RACE AND COGNITIVE SCORES
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The best decomposition assigns the majority of the US Black-White test score gap to environmental, cultural, and measurement factors, with the genetic contribution currently unestimated and unargued. Instrument bias (differential predictive validity) is small once education and item content are matched; the gap reduces substantially—often by 0.5–0.8 SD—when years of schooling, parental education, and test-taking motivation are statistically controlled. Culture (stereotype threat, test-wiseness, cultural content) and environment (neighborhood, nutrition, schooling quality) account for most of the remaining 0.2–0.4 SD, with a possible genetic share that could be zero or small but is not bounded by current data. The genetic share is not "known"; it is an upper-bound residual after all measured environmental factors are exhausted, which they are not.

MECHANISMS: The gap is ~1 SD (Black-White) on g-loaded tests; education controls reduce it to ~0.4 SD; SES controls reduce further to ~0.2–0.3 SD. The method of correlated vectors (which interprets larger gaps on more g-loaded items as evidence for genetic origin) is contested because it does not distinguish environmental from genetic mediation of g-loadings. Heritability within groups (0.5–0.8) does not imply between-group heritability; shared environment (schooling, parenting) explains ~20–40% of within-group variance in childhood and decays with age.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A genetic account argues that adoption studies (e.g., Minnesota Transracial Adoption) show Black children adopted into White families still score slightly below White norms, and admixture studies in some populations show a correlation between African ancestry and IQ after controlling for SES—suggesting a nonzero genetic component. This account also notes that g-loading and heritability of the gap are empirically correlated in some datasets, consistent with a genetic origin.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: A genetic share above ~0.1 would be falsified if (a) full environmental equalization (e.g., equivalent schooling from birth, identical parenting, elimination of stereotype threat) eliminated the gap entirely, or (b) polygenic scores for cognitive performance showed no predictive power for group differences once ancestry was partialled out in well-controlled adoption/admixture designs. Conversely, a genetic share below 0.05 would be falsified if adoption and admixture studies robustly showed persistence of a gap even after exhaustive environmental controls.

LANDING: The genetic contribution remains in the "not established" bin, and the burden of proof is on those who claim it is nonzero, given that environmental confounds are not exhausted.

Q02 — HERITABILITY OF TRAITS
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: Within-group heritability (h² ~ 0.5–0.8) implies that individual differences within a given population are substantially influenced by genetic variation, but it does not imply that a person's trait is fixed, that group differences are genetic, or that policy interventions are futile. For individual prediction, h² gives a population-level R² for genetic influences but does not allow accurate prediction for a specific person without their full genome and environment. For group-difference hypotheses, h² is logically irrelevant: groups can differ for environmental reasons even if traits are heritable within groups, or for genetic reasons even if h² is low. For policy, high h² does not mean interventions cannot shift means; it means that interventions that change environments may still shift the distribution, but the response to a uniform environment may be genetically heterogeneous.

MECHANISMS: h² is estimated from twin and family designs; it is environment-specific (e.g., lower in low-SES environments in some studies). The "scarr-rowe" interaction suggests heritability increases with SES; shared environment is larger in childhood and declines. Over-reading occurs when h² is mistaken for "biological fixedness" or "immutability," or when between-group heritability is inferred from within-group h² (the "heritability fallacy").

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A strong environmentalist account argues that h² is an artifact of unmeasured gene-environment correlation (passive, evocative, active) and that if environments were equalized, heritability would drop to near zero. This is supported by some cross-cultural studies showing lower heritability in more egalitarian or uniform environments.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "no policy implications" claim would be falsified if a high h² trait showed no response to any intervention (e.g., educational intervention fails to shift IQ means despite large environmental changes). The "no group implication" claim would be falsified if admixture or adoption studies consistently showed that group differences are entirely accounted for by genetic ancestry after exhaustive environmental controls.

LANDING: Within-group heritability implies variance decomposition, not causality or immutability; over-reading it leads to deterministic fallacies.

Q03 — SEX DIFFERENCES BEYOND THE ANODYNE
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The largest psychological sex differences—interest patterns (d ~ 0.8–1.2 for people-things), greater male variance (male SD ~ 1.1–1.2× female on many cognitive tests), and male visuospatial advantages (d ~ 0.3–0.6)—are large enough to matter at selection tails, especially in STEM, chess, and esports where the top 1–5% are overwhelmingly male. Culture/gatekeeping has been directly tested via matched-practice studies: when practice hours are equated, the male advantage in visuospatial tasks shrinks but does not vanish (e.g., in chess, female players with equal practice still underperform at the highest ranks). What has not been directly tested is a full elimination of all gatekeeping and stereotype threat from early childhood to elite levels, with matched practice volume and rank, and then observing whether the extreme tail gap disappears.

MECHANISMS: Interest differences drive differential practice volume and specialization; greater male variance produces more males at both tails. In chess, the gap at the top is larger than predicted by participation rates alone, suggesting either greater male variance or differential access to high-level coaching. In FPS/esports, reaction time (d ~ 0.3–0.5) and visuospatial tracking (d ~ 0.4) are male-advantaged, but culture (toxicity, less female-friendly environments) is a plausible suppressor of female entry and retention.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A pure culture/gatekeeping account holds that all differences would vanish if girls were given equal encouragement, coaching, and safe environments from age 5; the current gap is due to differential socialization, stereotype threat, and harassment. This is supported by the narrowing of the math gap in some countries and the rise of female chess players in some cohorts.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: A decisive test would be a randomized controlled trial where boys and girls are given identical training, coaching, and competitive exposure from early childhood, with no external differential treatment, and then the extreme-tail ratio is measured at age 20. If the male:female ratio at the top 1% remains > 5:1 despite equal practice and environment, that would knock out pure culture; if it drops to ~2:1, that would support culture as the ceiling mechanism.

LANDING: Large sex differences at tails are partly substrate-driven, but culture/gatekeeping has not been fully ruled out; matched-practice studies are the gold standard to distinguish them.

Q04 — CRIME STATISTICS BY DEMOGRAPHIC
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: After age structure is considered, the male-to-female homicide offender ratio is ~9:1 (with males comprising ~90% of offenders), and the Black-to-White homicide offender ratio is ~6–8:1 (Black rate ~20–30 per 100,000 vs White ~3–5 per 100,000 for homicide). After neighborhood controls (poverty, unemployment, family structure, policing intensity), the Black-White gap shrinks by about 50–70% but remains significant (e.g., Black homicide rate ~2–3× White rate after SES and neighborhood). That remainder is an unexplained residual, not an identified causal effect of race; it could reflect unmeasured neighborhood factors, historical disinvestment, or differential policing. The claim that rates are an artifact of police discretion is weak for homicide and victim surveys, because these do not depend on arrest discretion and show similar racial patterns.

MECHANISMS: Age structure is critical because crime is young-male-dominated (peak 15–25). After adjusting for age, the male gap remains large. Neighborhood controls—poverty, unemployment, single-parent households, and concentrated disadvantage—account for most of the race gap. The residual may reflect differential exposure to violence, informal social control, or historical segregation.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A strong discretion account argues that even homicide and victim surveys are affected by reporting bias, and that the racial gap in offending is an artifact of over-policing in Black neighborhoods, which leads to higher arrest rates and then higher official records, creating a feedback loop. This account emphasizes that NCVS offender descriptions may be biased by victim stereotypes.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "remainder is residual" claim would be falsified if a quasi-experimental design (e.g., court-mandated desegregation or mass incarceration reduction) showed that the residual gap disappears when neighborhood quality is equalized; alternatively, it would be supported if the residual persists after exhaustive controls. The artifact claim would be falsified if self-report offending surveys showed the same magnitude of racial differences as official data.

LANDING: Large demographic crime gaps are real, with neighborhood controls explaining much but not all; the remainder is unidentified, not identified as genetic or cultural.

Q05 — GENETICS AND GROUP DIFFERENCES
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: For ancestry-group differences on cognitive and behavioral traits, the genetic contribution is currently unknown and largely speculative; adoption studies (e.g., Minnesota Transracial Adoption) show mixed results—some show a small Black-White gap persisting in adoptive families, others show it shrinks to near zero. Admixture studies in populations like African Americans show some correlation between African ancestry and IQ after controlling for SES in some samples, but this correlation is small and often disappears when SES is more carefully measured. Polygenic scores (PGS) for educational attainment predict within-population differences but explain negligible variance between populations (R² < 0.01) and are confounded by ancestry-specific environmental correlations. Between-nation patterns (e.g., PISA scores vs GDP) are heavily confounded with schooling, nutrition, and test motivation. The error on any genetic estimate is enormous.

MECHANISMS: Within-group h² (~0.5–0.8) does not imply between-group h². PGS are population-specific; when applied across ancestry groups, they lose predictive power due to differences in allele frequencies and linkage disequilibrium. Adoption studies are confounded by selective placement and post-adoption environment; admixture studies are confounded by SES and discrimination correlated with ancestry.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A genetic account points to the consistency of the Black-White gap across adoption studies and to admixture correlations as evidence for a nonzero genetic contribution. It also notes that PGS for IQ, when properly calibrated, show some ancestry-related differences, and that the gap is more g-loaded, which is consistent with a genetic origin in some models.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: A nonzero genetic contribution would be falsified if (a) full environmental equalization (identical schooling, parenting, nutrition from birth) eliminated the gap entirely, or (b) well-powered admixture studies showed zero correlation between ancestry and cognitive trait after exhaustive SES controls. Conversely, a genetic contribution above zero would be supported if adoption and admixture studies robustly showed persistence after exhaustive environmental controls.

LANDING: The genetic contribution is not established; current evidence is weak, confounded, and error-prone; it remains speculative.

Q06 — TRANS IDENTITIES, YOUTH CARE, SPORTS
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The shift in referral sex ratios (from male-predominant to female-predominant in youth, particularly in some European clinics) and the narrowing of youth medicalization in European health systems after evidence reviews indicate that pediatric medical transition should be handled with caution, especially for adolescents with psychiatric comorbidities or desistance risk. Social transition in schools is lower-stakes but still empirical: early social transition may increase persistence of gender dysphoria, though it reduces acute distress. For sports, male performance advantage in speed, strength, and endurance is not fully removed by typical testosterone suppression (e.g., in running, swimming, weightlifting), so female-category sports require either exclusion of transwomen with male puberty or a very low testosterone threshold for a long duration; spaces (bathrooms, locker rooms) are less well-studied and rest on privacy and safety claims. The strongest affirmation-first claim is that gender-affirming care reduces depression and suicide risk in carefully selected adolescents; the weakest is that all adolescents with gender dysphoria benefit from medical transition without rigorous screening.

MECHANISMS: European reviews (e.g., Sweden, Finland, UK) found low-quality evidence for puberty blockers and cross-sex hormones; they recommended psychological support first. Desistance rates in youth vary widely (2–30% in studies), with psychiatric comorbidity (autism, depression, anxiety) being common. Sports advantage: transwomen after 1–2 years of testosterone suppression retain ~5–10% advantage in running and swimming, and larger advantages in strength-based sports.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: An affirmation-first account holds that youth medicalization is life-saving for a small subset of severely dysphoric adolescents, that desistance is overestimated due to poor follow-up, and that sports advantages are trivial after adequate suppression; it emphasizes the mental health benefits of social affirmation and the low risk of regret.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The caution on medical transition would be falsified if long-term RCTs showed that early puberty blockers improved adult mental health and reduced suicide without significant bone density or fertility effects. The sports exclusion would be falsified if transwomen with 2+ years of suppression showed no performance advantage over ciswomen in elite competition. The social transition caution would be falsified if early social transition was shown to reduce long-term dysphoria without increasing persistence.

LANDING: Youth transition requires caution and high-quality evidence; sports have a retained male advantage; social transition is less risky but not zero-risk.

Q07 — IMMIGRATION AND GROUP OUTCOMES
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: Immigrant outcomes are predominantly driven by selection (e.g., educational and skill filters) and source-country human capital, with host policy (welfare generosity, labor market integration) and discrimination playing smaller but nonzero roles. In the U.S., Asian immigrants have higher average earnings and test scores than natives, driven by selection (e.g., H-1B visas, family reunification with high-skilled relatives); Mexican and Central American immigrants have lower outcomes, driven by lower source-country education and less selective migration. In Europe, Turkish and Moroccan immigrants in Germany/Netherlands show lower outcomes, partly due to lower source-country human capital and discrimination. The datasets that separate these are longitudinal studies of immigrant cohorts (e.g., U.S. Census, German Socio-Economic Panel) that track education, earnings, and crime over generations; they show that second-generation outcomes converge toward host-country means but not fully. If decomposition is taken seriously, skill filters (e.g., points-based systems) improve average outcomes, while asylum design should focus on refugee status and humanitarian needs, not economic selection.

MECHANISMS: Selection explains ~50–70% of variance in immigrant earnings; source-country human capital explains another ~10–20%; discrimination explains ~5–15% (e.g., audit studies show callback gaps). Host policy (e.g., welfare, language training) explains ~5–10%. Second-generation convergence is strong: by third generation, most groups approach native means.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A discrimination-heavy account holds that structural racism and xenophobia are the primary drivers of immigrant disadvantage, that selection and source-country factors are overemphasized, and that host policy and discrimination explain the bulk of differences. It points to audit studies and wage gaps that persist after controlling for education.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "selection/source-country dominant" account would be falsified if immigrants with identical pre-migration skills and from the same source country had wildly different outcomes across host countries due solely to policy/discrimination. The discrimination account would be falsified if within-country variation in immigrant outcomes was entirely explained by source-country human capital.

LANDING: Selection and source-country human capital dominate; host policy and discrimination matter but are secondary; skill filters improve average outcomes.

Q08 — ISLAM COMPARED WITH OTHER RELIGIONS
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: On measurable items like blasphemy enforcement, apostasy norms, cousin-marriage prevalence, and sex-role attitudes, Islam in Western countries differs from other large religions (e.g., Christianity, Judaism, Hinduism) after controlling for composition, but the differences are smaller than often claimed and vary by origin country. Muslim immigrants in Europe show more conservative attitudes on gender roles and homosexuality than Christian or non-religious natives, but this is partly explained by lower education, rural origin, and age. Cousin-marriage prevalence is higher among some Muslim groups (e.g., Pakistani in UK) but is cultural, not Islamic doctrine. Support for sharia and blasphemy laws is higher among Muslims than other groups, but it declines with integration and education. The "it's just like every religion" claim is accurate for core piety (prayer, charity) and for discrimination experience, but inaccurate for specific legal norms (apostasy, blasphemy) and marriage patterns.

MECHANISMS: Composition controls (education, age, rural origin, income) reduce but do not eliminate differences. Muslim attitudes on gender roles are ~0.3–0.5 SD more conservative than Christian counterparts, but this is comparable to conservative Christian groups in the U.S. (e.g., evangelicals). Cousin-marriage is more prevalent in Pakistan and Middle East due to cultural tradition, not Islamic doctrine. The asymmetry in Western institutions (less criticism of Islam) is a claim about elite discourse and is not directly tested by survey data.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A "no meaningful difference" account holds that all religious groups have conservative factions, that Muslim differences are solely due to lower SES and rural origin, and that the asymmetry claim is exaggerated or false; it emphasizes that Muslims in the West are often more liberal than Muslims in their home countries.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "doctrine matters" claim would be falsified if Muslim immigrants from highly secular countries (e.g., Albania, Turkey) showed no difference from Christian immigrants on sharia or apostasy attitudes. The "just like other religions" claim would be falsified if Muslim attitudes on apostasy and blasphemy remained significantly more conservative after exhaustive controls.

LANDING: Islam differs from other religions on some legal and social norms, but differences are moderated by integration and SES; the asymmetry claim is plausible but not robustly tested.

Q09 — POLICING AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The best causal evidence linking policing intensity to violent crime comes from quasi-experimental designs like "hot-spot" policing, which show that increased police presence in high-crime areas reduces violent crime by ~5–15% within the intervention period. The 2020–2022 homicide spike in the U.S. correlates with policing pullbacks (e.g., reduced stops, arrests, and proactive policing) after George Floyd, but causality is not definitively established because other factors (gun sales, pandemic stress, social unrest) also changed. Racial disparity in stops and in offending are two different quantities: stops are an output of policing decisions, offending is an input; they should be analyzed separately. A "policing pullback raised homicide" account would be falsified if cities with no pullback also saw similar homicide increases, or if the increase was explained entirely by gun sales and pandemic stress.

MECHANISMS: Hot-spot policing RCTs show consistent reductions in crime, with effect sizes of 5–15%. The 2020 homicide increase was largest in cities that reduced proactive stops; however, many cities that maintained policing also saw increases. Victim demand for policing in high-violence neighborhoods is high (e.g., surveys show support for more police in Black neighborhoods).

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A "policing reduction not causal" account holds that the homicide spike was driven by gun sales (record highs in 2020), pandemic social disruption, and reduced court activity, not by policing pullbacks; it points to cities with no pullback that still saw spikes.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The causal account would be falsified if a city with a sharp policing pullback had no homicide increase, or if a city with increased policing had a homicide spike. The non-causal account would be falsified if, after controlling for gun sales and pandemic factors, a strong correlation between policing pullback and homicide remained.

LANDING: Policing intensity reduces violent crime with moderate effect; racial disparity in stops and offending are distinct; the policing pullback-homicide link is plausible but not proven.

Q10 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION AND MERIT
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The best data (e.g., from the Texas Top 10% plan, California's ban, and law school mismatch studies) show that race-conscious admissions increase minority representation but produce mixed effects on downstream performance: some studies find mismatch (lower grades, bar pass rates) for students admitted under preferences, while others find no mismatch or even benefits. The tradeoff is that group means on predictors (e.g., SAT, GPA) differ, so selecting for diversity means selecting lower-predictor-scoring applicants; the outcome on actual job or course performance is domain-dependent—in law, bar pass rates show some mismatch; in medical school, board scores show small mismatch; in engineering, graduation rates show mixed results. The tradeoff is justified if (a) the institution values diversity for educational or social reasons, and (b) the predicted performance difference is small or can be remediated with support. Class-based or test-based selection would reduce the diversity benefit but might improve average predictor scores.

MECHANISMS: Mismatch occurs when students are placed in schools where their credentials are below the median, leading to lower grades and higher dropout; evidence is strongest in law and military academies. However, some studies (e.g., Duke, University of Michigan) show no mismatch for Black students in STEM. The tradeoff is between average predicted performance and group representation.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A merit-based account holds that race preferences are unfair and inefficient, that mismatch is widespread, and that class-based or test-based selection would produce similar diversity with better outcomes. It points to California's ban on race-based admissions as a natural experiment showing that minority graduation rates improved after the ban in some institutions.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "mismatch is real" claim would be falsified if, in a well-controlled study, minority students admitted under preferences performed as well as or better than peers with similar credentials. The "justified" claim would be falsified if mismatch consistently lowered minority professional success and if diversity benefits were empirically trivial.

LANDING: Race preferences have tradeoffs; mismatch exists in some domains but not all; justification depends on institutional values and remediation capacity.

Q11 — ELITE FPS / ESPORTS SEX GAP
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: Female representation at the Tier-1 ceiling in FPS is near zero (~0.1–1% at top 100). Competing accounts have been tested with matched rank and hours: at the same rank, female players have similar or sometimes higher hours to reach that rank, suggesting no practice deficit once in the pool. However, the selection into the pool is heavily male, so the extreme tail gap is driven by differential participation rather than performance per hour. Culture/toxicity/gatekeeping has been tested via survey and interview studies showing higher harassment rates for female players, and some experiments show that female-identified accounts receive more toxicity. The substrate/selection account (greater male variance, psychomotor tails) has not been directly tested against culture at matched practice volume and rank because you cannot randomly assign sex and environment. To knock out substrate in favor of culture, a study would need to show that, with equal encouragement, coaching, and harassment-free environments from early childhood, the male:female ratio at the top 1% drops to below 2:1.

MECHANISMS: Greater male variance (SD ratio ~1.1) predicts more males at extreme tails; psychomotor tests (reaction time ~20–40 ms male advantage, visuospatial tracking ~0.3 SD) contribute small but cumulative advantages. Participation rates explain ~80–90% of the top representation gap; the remaining ~10–20% could be due to variance or culture.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A pure culture account holds that the gap is entirely due to harassment, lack of role models, and differential encouragement; it points to the rise of female-only leagues and the fact that in countries with more egalitarian culture (e.g., Scandinavia), the gap is smaller but still large.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: A decisive test would be a long-term intervention where girls are given equal access, coaching, and harassment-free environments from age 6, and the top-100 ratio is measured at age 20. If the ratio remains >5:1, substrate is supported; if it drops to <2:1, culture is supported.

LANDING: Participation accounts for most of the FPS gap; culture and substrate both contribute, but their relative weight is unknown; a full cultural test has not been done.

Q12 — IAT AND IMPLICIT BIAS AS POLICY
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The IAT is not a valid policy instrument for institutional redesign because its test-retest reliability is low (r ~0.5–0.6), its predictive validity for real-world discrimination is small (r ~0.1–0.2), and its construct mapping (what it measures) is contested. It is useful for research on automatic associations but not for diagnosing individual bias or justifying mandatory training. If institutions want to detect bias, better replacements include behavioral audits (e.g., callback rates in hiring), disparate impact analysis on objective outcomes, and structured interviews with standardized scoring. For current uses to be justified, the IAT would need (a) test-retest reliability >0.8, (b) predictive validity for discrimination >0.3, and (c) a clear and uncontested interpretation of the score as stable prejudice.

MECHANISMS: IAT measures reaction-time differences; these are influenced by salience, familiarity, and task strategy, not just prejudice. Meta-analyses (e.g., Oswald et al.) show that IAT predicts discrimination only weakly, and in many domains not at all. Training based on IAT often does not change behavior.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A pro-IAT account holds that IAT predicts subtle discrimination (microbehaviors, nonverbal cues) better than overt behavior, and that its low predictive validity is due to measurement error and context dependence; it argues that aggregate studies show significant, though small, predictive effects.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "not valid" claim would be falsified if a large-scale RCT showed that IAT-based training reduced objective discriminatory outcomes (e.g., hiring disparities, promotion gaps) by >20%. The "replace with audits" claim would be falsified if behavioral audits were found to be more biased or less sensitive than IAT.

LANDING: IAT is not suitable for policy; behavioral audits and outcome-based disparate impact are better.

Q13 — FAMILY STRUCTURE AND CHILD OUTCOMES
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: After income is considered, children in stable two-parent homes still show better outcomes (e.g., schooling, crime, adult earnings) by about 0.2–0.4 SD compared to single-mother homes. The remainder is a mix of selection (parents who stay married differ on traits like conscientiousness, education, and mental health) and a causal family-structure effect (father absence, instability, reduced supervision). The causal remainder is estimated at ~0.1–0.2 SD in quasi-experimental designs (e.g., divorce shocks, death of a parent). Policy implications: if a causal remainder is real, interventions that reduce family instability (e.g., parenting programs, child support, stable housing) could improve child outcomes, but they are less effective than income transfers.

MECHANISMS: Income explains ~30–50% of the gap; selection explains another ~20–30% (e.g., parental education, IQ, mental health). Causal mechanisms include reduced supervision, less cognitive stimulation, and stress. Studies using sibling fixed effects or divorce shocks show smaller but significant effects.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A selection-only account holds that all remaining differences are due to unmeasured parental traits (e.g., impulsivity, intelligence) that cause both divorce and poor child outcomes; it points to twin and adoption studies showing that genetic confounds explain much of the association.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "causal remainder is real" claim would be falsified if a randomized intervention that kept families together (e.g., free marriage counseling) had no effect on child outcomes. The "selection-only" claim would be falsified if quasi-experimental studies (e.g., sudden father death) showed persistent child outcome gaps.

LANDING: Family structure has a small causal effect beyond income and selection; policy should address both.

Q14 — SEX PAY AND OCCUPATIONAL GAPS
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The raw sex pay gap (~80% female/male in the U.S.) shrinks to ~92–96% after controlling for occupation, hours, experience, and career discontinuity; about 60–70% of the gap is hours and occupation, ~10–20% is preference (e.g., choosing lower-paying specialties or part-time work), and ~5–15% is discrimination at the offer or promotion stage. Occupational sorting tracks interest differences (people vs things, d ~1.0) in many datasets, and these interests are stable and partially heritable. A "mostly sorting" account would be falsified if, in a well-controlled experiment, women and men with identical qualifications, preferences, and career histories were offered systematically different salaries within the same firm. A "mostly bias" account would be falsified if, after eliminating explicit bias, the gap remained large due to hours and occupational choices.

MECHANISMS: Hours worked: men work ~2–5 more hours/week on average; occupation: women cluster in education, healthcare, and social sciences, while men cluster in finance, tech, and trades; career discontinuity: women take more years off for childcare, reducing experience. Discrimination is documented in audit studies (e.g., same resume with female name gets fewer callbacks) and in firm-level analyses (e.g., women promoted less often).

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A bias-heavy account holds that discrimination is the primary driver, that occupational sorting is driven by gender norms and discrimination, and that once bias is eliminated, the gap would disappear. It points to the fact that even within the same occupation, women earn less.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "mostly sorting" account would be falsified if a country with very strong anti-discrimination laws (e.g., Iceland) showed a near-zero gap. The "mostly bias" account would be falsified if, even in discrimination-free environments (e.g., some tech firms with blind hiring), the gap remained large.

LANDING: Hours and occupation dominate; preference and discrimination contribute smaller shares.

Q15 — USE OF FORCE AND RACIAL DISPARITY
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: For stops, the right denominator is population or encounter rates—Black Americans are stopped at higher rates than Whites per capita, but per encounter, rates are more similar. For shootings, the right denominator is armed/suspect status at the moment of encounter; after controlling for armed status, some studies find no racial disparity in lethal force, while others find a small (but often not significant) disparity favoring Black suspects (i.e., officers are less likely to shoot unarmed Black suspects than White suspects). The best evidence on racial bias in lethal force after the encounter has begun comes from Fryer (2019) and the NBER study, which find no racial disparity in police shootings after controlling for armed status, but find disparities in non-lethal force (e.g., handcuffing, pushing). What remains unresolved is the denominator debate: per population vs per encounter vs per crime rate—each gives different answers.

MECHANISMS: Stops are more discretionary than shootings; non-lethal force shows larger disparities than lethal force. Fryer's analysis used data from Houston, Florida, and Texas, finding that unarmed Blacks were more likely to be subject to non-lethal force but not more likely to be shot. Other studies (e.g., Washington Post) show that unarmed Blacks are killed at higher rates than unarmed Whites per population, but when armed status is controlled, the disparity shrinks.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A bias account holds that the denominator should be population or crime rate, not armed status, because armed status is itself influenced by prior policing; it points to the raw numbers showing higher rates of unarmed Black deaths and argues that controlling for armed status masks structural bias.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "no bias in lethal force" claim would be falsified if a large-scale study with body camera data showed that, after controlling for armed status and suspect behavior, officers were more likely to shoot Black suspects. The "bias exists" claim would be falsified if the disparity disappeared under exhaustive controls and the residual was zero.

LANDING: Disparities in stops are larger than in lethal force; denominator matters; lethal force bias is unresolved but likely smaller than non-lethal.

Q16 — ELECTORAL INTEGRITY
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: Voter ID laws have a documented turnout cost of ~1–3 percentage points for minority and low-income voters in some studies, but other studies find no effect. Mail-ballot fraud is rare (e.g., <0.001% of votes in audits), but procedural tightness (chain of custody, signature verification) is important for confidence. Same-day registration increases turnout by ~3–5 points but may increase administrative burden. Evidence is too thin for claims that mail voting is highly fraudulent or that voter ID has no cost; both sides overstate their case. The integrity benefit of ID is in public confidence, not fraud prevention.

MECHANISMS: Voter ID reduces turnout by increasing the cost of voting (e.g., obtaining ID), especially for those without driver's licenses. Mail-ballot fraud is rare because of signature verification and tracking. Same-day registration increases turnout but may lead to provisional ballots and longer lines.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A voter ID advocate argues that ID is necessary for election integrity, that costs are small (most voters have ID), and that public confidence outweighs any minimal turnout reduction. A mail-ballot critic argues that chain-of-custody issues and harvesting risk justify restrictions.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "low fraud" claim would be falsified if a large-scale audit found >0.01% fraud in mail ballots. The "cost is small" claim would be falsified if a well-controlled study found >5% turnout reduction in ID laws.

LANDING: Fraud is rare; ID costs are small but non-zero; evidence is thin on both extremes.

Q17 — VACCINE RISK, WHEN THE OBJECTION IS EMPIRICAL
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: Myocarditis risk after mRNA vaccines is clear: in young males (12–29), risk is ~1–5 per 100,000 doses, usually mild and self-limiting; infection-acquired immunity provides broader and longer protection than vaccine-acquired immunity but with higher severe disease risk; all-cause mortality in specific windows is lower in vaccinated than unvaccinated in all age groups; original-trial endpoints (efficacy) were solid, but later claims (e.g., waning, booster benefit) evolved with data. Institutions overclaimed certainty on booster necessity and on the absence of rare side effects early on, but the core safety profile is robust. Open questions: duration of myocarditis sequelae, optimal interval for boosters, and vaccine efficacy against new variants.

MECHANISMS: Myocarditis is triggered by immune response to spike protein; risk is age- and sex-dependent. Infection-acquired immunity has higher antibody breadth but higher severe disease risk. All-cause mortality data from large cohorts show vaccination reduces death.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A vaccine-skeptical account holds that myocarditis risk is underreported, that boosters have negative benefit-risk for young people, and that institutions suppressed legitimate safety concerns. It points to VAERS data and some European studies.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "safety is robust" claim would be falsified if a large cohort study found that myocarditis led to significant long-term heart damage in >1% of cases. The "institutions overclaimed" claim would be falsified if early booster recommendations were shown to be optimal in retrospect.

LANDING: Safety is robust; myocarditis risk is real but small; some overclaim occurred.

Q18 — CLIMATE POLICY VS CLIMATE SCIENCE
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: The science is robust on warming, GHG driver, and sea-level rise; it is less robust on regional impacts, climate sensitivity (likely 2.5–4°C per doubling of CO2, with tails), and specific event attribution for disasters (e.g., hurricanes). Policy disputes—carbon price level, timeline, ban stacks, adaptation mix—are not physical-science disputes; they involve economics (discount rates), ethics (intergenerational equity), and feasibility. The robust science supports rapid emissions reduction, but the optimal policy mix is contested and should not be sold as settled. Disaster attribution is increasingly robust for heatwaves and floods, but less so for storms.

MECHANISMS: Climate sensitivity has a long tail (uncertainty around 1.5–4.5°C); regional projections have large model spread. Discount rates (2–5%) heavily influence optimal carbon price. Adaptation (sea walls, crop changes) is under-researched.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A strong policy advocate argues that the science mandates immediate aggressive action (e.g., carbon tax >$100/ton, rapid phase-out of fossil fuels), and that any delay is unjustified. A gradualist account argues that technology and adaptation will reduce costs.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "policy is contested" claim would be falsified if a consensus economic model showed that one specific policy (e.g., carbon tax) dominated all others across all plausible parameters. The "science is robust" claim would be falsified if climate sensitivity was found to be <2°C or >5°C with high probability.

LANDING: Science is robust on warming and driver; policy is contested and not reducible to physics.

Q19 — POVERTY: STRUCTURAL VS BEHAVIORAL
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: U.S. poverty persistence is about 50–70% structural (low wages, neighborhood, discrimination, thin safety net) and 30–50% behavioral/human capital (hours worked, skill, delayed childbearing, family stability, time preference). Structural interventions like the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) and housing vouchers have moved the needle modestly (e.g., EITC reduces poverty by ~5–10%); behavioral interventions like job training and family planning also show modest effects (e.g., Nurse-Family Partnership reduces child poverty). A structure-only story would be falsified if, after massive transfers and housing subsidies, poverty did not decline; a behavior-only story would be falsified if, after intensive job training and family planning, poverty did not decline.

MECHANISMS: Structural factors explain wage stagnation, segregation, and discrimination; behavioral factors explain differential rates of labor force participation, marriage, and education. Interventions: EITC (structural) increases work and reduces poverty; early childhood education (behavioral/human capital) improves long-term outcomes.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A structure-only account holds that all poverty is due to discrimination, lack of jobs, and inadequate transfers; it points to high poverty rates despite work in low-wage jobs. A behavior-only account holds that poverty is due to poor choices (dropping out, single motherhood, drug use); it points to ethnic immigrant success.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "mixed" account would be falsified if one category (structure or behavior) consistently explained >80% of variance in poverty across all groups and regions.

LANDING: Poverty is a mix; both structure and behavior matter; interventions target both.

Q20 — DISPARATE IMPACT AS PROOF
--------------------------------
DIRECT ANSWER: Disparate impact is evidence of discrimination when (a) the instrument or process is not job-relevant, (b) the impact is not explained by group differences in the relevant trait, or (c) the process is avoidable and less-biased alternatives exist. It is evidence of a real group difference on a job-relevant trait when the predictor is validated and the group difference is consistent with known population differences (e.g., physical strength for firefighting). The rule to decide: use the "job-relevant, validated, no less-biased alternative" criterion—if the predictor meets that, impact is not discrimination; if it fails, impact is suspect. The burden of proof is on the user of the instrument to show validity.

MECHANISMS: Disparate impact is a statistical signal; it requires a causal theory of why the gap exists. If the gap is due to a valid job-relevant trait, then impact is not evidence of discrimination. If the gap is due to bias in the test itself (e.g., cultural content), then it is evidence.

STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT: A strict disparity account holds that any group-differential impact should be treated as discrimination unless the instrument is proven to be perfectly job-relevant and unbiased; it shifts the burden to the defender. A strict merit account holds that any validated instrument is fair, regardless of impact.

WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER: The "validated predictor" claim would be falsified if a predictor that was validated in one setting failed to predict performance in another setting for minority groups (differential validity). The "impact is evidence" claim would be falsified if the impact was shown to be entirely due to a valid, unavoidable group trait.

LANDING: Disparate impact is evidence, not proof; validity and alternatives determine whether it is discrimination.
