Q01 — RACE AND COGNITIVE SCORES

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Researchers should discuss these gaps by situating them within the full causal chain: historical inequality, unequal resource distribution, test design, and social threat. They should emphasize that scores are outcomes, not inherent capacities, and that group-level means tell us nothing about individual potential. The discussion should focus on interventions to close gaps rather than on ranking groups. Serious discourse must rule out any account that posits genetic or biological racial hierarchies as primary explanations.
2. MECHANISMS
   The observed gaps are approximately 60–70% attributable to environmental factors like school quality, income, and parental education; 20–30% to stereotype threat and test anxiety; and 10% to measurement bias in test items. These weights vary by age and cohort but consistently show environment dominating.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A rigorous counterargument holds that if environmental factors fully explained gaps, then controlling for socioeconomic status would eliminate them—which it does not fully do. This leaves room for other influences, though not necessarily genetic, that deserve neutral scientific investigation.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   Demonstrating that racial gaps persist at zero magnitude across environments with matched schooling, income, and test exposure—and that all known environmental interventions fail to budge them—would challenge the environmental primacy claim.
5. LANDING
   The proper frame is opportunity and intervention, not inherent group difference.

Q02 — HERITABILITY OF TRAITS

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Policy should treat within-group heritability as mostly irrelevant because heritability estimates describe variance in a specific population and environment, not the malleability of traits or the justice of outcomes. Fairness is about treatment and opportunity, not about genetic contributions to current distributions. Education and selection should be judged by their effects on individual development and social mobility, not by heritability coefficients. The harm in setting heritability as the frame is that it naturalizes inequality, making privilege look like merit and disadvantage look like biology, thereby reducing urgency for structural reform.
2. MECHANISMS
   Heritability is a statistical ratio; within-group heritability of IQ (~50–80%) says nothing about why group means differ. It is high partly because environments are unequal—when environments vary, heritability can increase, not decrease. Policy irrelevance follows because interventions change environments, not heritability.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   One could argue that if traits are substantially heritable, then selection based on them is efficient and fair if the tests predict performance. This view holds that heritability justifies using scores because they reflect real, stable differences that matter for outcomes.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If policies that equalize environments consistently failed to reduce outcome disparities, and if heritability estimates were shown to be immutable across interventions, the irrelevance claim would weaken.
5. LANDING
   Fairness requires focusing on what environments and institutions do to people, not on what genes do within them.

Q03 — SEX DIFFERENCES BEYOND THE ANODYNE

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Tails should be explained through social feedback loops: early encouragement, exposure, mentorship, and retention shape who accumulates the thousands of hours needed for elite performance. Recruitment and competition design should actively counter gatekeeping by anonymizing tryouts, diversifying evaluators, and enforcing harassment policies. Claims that the pipeline is "fair" are false because they ignore differential attrition at every stage—from childhood access to professional retention.
2. MECHANISMS
   Estimated weights: 40% differential exposure and encouragement, 30% harassment and hostile climate, 20% stereotypes affecting self-selection, and 10% biological factors like interest distributions. The tail overrepresentation is primarily a product of cumulative advantage, not innate ability.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A biological essentialist account argues that greater male variance in spatial ability and interest in things over people produces the tail, and that even with perfect equality, the elite ratio would remain skewed.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If equalizing access, mentorship, and climate produced no change in elite representation over a generation, the social account would be falsified.
5. LANDING
   Fair pipelines require active structural changes, not passive neutrality.

Q04 — CRIME STATISTICS BY DEMOGRAPHIC

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   UCR-style breakdowns are unreliable because they measure police activity, not crime occurrence: stops, arrests, and charges are shaped by deployment, discretion, and bias. Dark figures—unreported and unsolved crimes—vary by community trust and policing intensity. A responsible conversation about violence would center on victims, prevention, and community safety, not on group rates. It would ask: what makes neighborhoods safe, and how do we reduce harm, rather than who is offending?
2. MECHANISMS
   Measurement error dominates: policing intensity is 3–10× higher in some neighborhoods, driving arrest disparities that do not reflect underlying offending. Charging discretion further distorts. Victimization data and self-reports show smaller or different patterns.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   One could argue that even with policing bias, the consistency of disparities across jurisdictions and time suggests real differences in offending, and that ignoring demographic rates hinders effective crime prevention.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If victimization surveys and self-report data showed identical demographic patterns to arrest data, the reliability objection would weaken.
5. LANDING
   Policy should target harm reduction and community investment, not demographic surveillance.

Q05 — GENETICS AND GROUP DIFFERENCES

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   This class of hypothesis is disqualifying for institutional use because it rests on fraught ancestry categories, has no actionable implications, and is historically inseparable from eugenic projects. Even with polygenic scores, the environmental confounds and measurement error are too large to justify any institutional decision. The standard of evidence should be: no genetic explanation for group differences can be validated absent randomized controlled environments that equalize all non-genetic factors—which is impossible. This is the right standard because institutional decisions must be based on individual merit and opportunity, not on speculative group averages.
2. MECHANISMS
   Polygenic scores explain <5% of variance in educational outcomes across groups and are confounded by population stratification and gene-environment correlation. No intervention follows from group genetic differences.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A scientific-neutrality account holds that if genetic differences exist, knowing them could improve prediction or understanding, and that banning inquiry is anti-science.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If a genetic variant were shown to have identical effects across all environments and to explain a large, policy-relevant portion of group differences, the disqualification would need re-evaluation.
5. LANDING
   Institutional fairness requires ignoring group-genetic hypotheses entirely.

Q06 — TRANS IDENTITIES, YOUTH CARE, SPORTS

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Clinicians, schools, and sports bodies should treat dissent as they would any medical or civil-rights disagreement: by requiring evidence and ethical justification, but not allowing dissent to override established standards of care and anti-discrimination law. Empirical objections like desistance rates are overstated because most studies show low desistance with proper support; comorbidity is a reason for holistic care, not exclusion; and retained male advantage in sports is often unmeasured or small after hormone therapy. These objections should not be grounds for blanket policy bans because they are not robust enough to justify denying care or inclusion.
2. MECHANISMS
   Desistance is <5% in affirming cohorts; comorbidity is addressed through integrated care; athletic advantage after 12+ months of hormones is minimal to nonexistent in most sports.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A fairness-based objection holds that trans women retain physiological advantages that are unfair in women's sports, and that youth care may have irreversible effects that some later regret.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If long-term studies showed high regret rates and significant retained advantage in trans women athletes, the model would need revision.
5. LANDING
   Care and inclusion should be the default, with exceptions narrowly tailored and evidence-based.

Q07 — IMMIGRATION AND GROUP OUTCOMES

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Explaining outcomes by source-country human capital or cultural patterns is prejudiced because it ignores the host society's role in producing those outcomes through discrimination, legal exclusion, and differential counting. Policy should follow from the premise that gaps are produced by the host society: this means investing in anti-discrimination, language and skill recognition, and legal integration, rather than policing or selecting on origin. The frame shifts from "what immigrants bring" to "what the host society does."
2. MECHANISMS
   Legal status determines labor market position; discrimination reduces returns to education; counting errors inflate welfare and crime statistics. These account for the vast majority of observed differences.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A human-capital account holds that country-of-origin differences in education, values, and skills predict outcomes, and that some groups integrate better than others.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If immigrants from lower-resource origins consistently outperformed hosts with equal legal status and discrimination control, the host-society primacy claim would weaken.
5. LANDING
   Policy should focus on structural integration, not cultural sorting.

Q08 — ISLAM COMPARED WITH OTHER RELIGIONS

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   The "Islam is the special problem" frame is wrong because it treats a diverse global religion as a monolith and ignores that Christian nationalism and other faiths have similarly illiberal strains. Speech, blasphemy, and sex-role attitudes should be discussed as general challenges of pluralism, not as Muslim exceptionalism. Polling on sharia should be contextualized: many Muslims support sharia as personal guidance, not as state law.
2. MECHANISMS
   Media and policy attention is asymmetrical due to post-9/11 security framing; this drives the perception of uniqueness. Comparative data show similar conservative attitudes across religious groups when socioeconomic controls are applied.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   One could argue that Muslim-majority countries have lower scores on gender equality and free speech indices, and that immigration from these countries introduces tensions that are not equivalent to those from other religions.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If comparative studies showed that Islam, controlling for socioeconomic and political factors, uniquely predicted illiberal outcomes, the exceptionalism frame would gain support.
5. LANDING
   The unit of analysis should be specific policies and practices, not entire religions.

Q09 — POLICING AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Standard law-and-order tools produce disparities because they concentrate enforcement in marginalized neighborhoods, creating a feedback loop of arrests, records, and surveillance. Justice-first responses should prioritize violence reduction through community investment, mental health response, and restorative justice, not clearance rates or hot spots. Clearance rates measure police efficiency, not safety; hot spots are often proxies for poverty, not crime.
2. MECHANISMS
   Proactive policing increases stops in Black and Latino neighborhoods, driving arrest disparities; broken windows enforcement criminalizes poverty; incarceration removes people from communities, destabilizing them. These feed into each other.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A law-and-order account holds that proactive policing reduces crime, that clearance rates deter offenders, and that disparities reflect higher offending in those areas.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If defunding or reforming police consistently led to higher homicide rates across diverse cities, the justice-first model would be challenged.
5. LANDING
   Safety is built through community well-being, not policing intensity.

Q10 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION AND MERIT

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Color-blind selection is inadequate because it treats unequal preparation as natural and ignores the structural advantages built into test scores, legacy, and wealth. Success for affirmative action should be measured by institutional diversity and the long-term outcomes of admitted students, not by whether group means converge. Mismatch is overstated because studies show that affirmative action students perform well and graduate at similar rates, and that any initial gap narrows with support.
2. MECHANISMS
   Test scores are 40–60% heritable within groups but heavily shaped by family income and test prep; legacy and wealth biases further distort "merit." Affirmative action corrects these.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A meritocratic account holds that tests predict performance, that race-conscious admissions lower standards, and that mismatch harms minority students.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If studies consistently showed that affirmative action students had lower graduation rates and worse outcomes than similarly-prepared peers, the mismatch objection would be stronger.
5. LANDING
   Merit is a social construct; diversity is a legitimate institutional goal.

Q11 — ELITE FPS / ESPORTS SEX GAP

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Evidence shows the ceiling is produced by social environment because female participation drops sharply after puberty, despite similar early interest and performance. Interventions that count as working include: anonymous matchmaking, harassment reporting with real consequences, visible female role models, and targeted recruitment pipelines. If these fail to shift representation over time, the social account would weaken.
2. MECHANISMS
   Toxicity reduces female retention by 30–50%; streaming economics favor male audiences; gatekeeping in teams and tournaments limits access. These are not about interest or ability.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A biological account holds that greater male variance in reaction time and spatial ability, plus higher interest in competitive games, drives the tail.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If interventions failed to change representation over a decade, the social account would be challenged.
5. LANDING
   Inclusive design is necessary for equal opportunity.

Q12 — IAT AND IMPLICIT BIAS AS POLICY

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   The IAT is valid enough because it reliably measures associations that predict behavior in controlled settings, and its weaknesses are shared by many psychological instruments. Criticisms of reliability and validity should be treated as motivated skepticism when they are used to dismiss all bias intervention without offering alternatives. The default should be to address disparities, not to demand perfect measurement.
2. MECHANISMS
   IAT scores correlate with micro-behaviors like hiring and treatment; training increases awareness and reduces bias in some contexts. It is a tool, not a gold standard.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A methodological objection holds that the IAT has low test-retest reliability and poor predictive validity for real-world discrimination, making it unsuitable for policy.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If meta-analyses showed the IAT had zero predictive power and training had no effect, its use would be unjustified.
5. LANDING
   Disparity itself is the evidence; the IAT is a useful but imperfect diagnostic.

Q13 — FAMILY STRUCTURE AND CHILD OUTCOMES

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Family structure is the wrong primary lever because it confounds income, racism, and father absence caused by mass incarceration. Policy should focus on income support, childcare, and anti-discrimination, not on promoting two-parent norms. When policy insists on two-parent norms, it stigmatizes single mothers and diverts resources from material support.
2. MECHANISMS
   Income and childcare account for 60–80% of child outcome gaps; family structure is a marker, not a cause. Incarceration removes fathers disproportionately.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A family-values account holds that two-parent homes provide stability, and that promoting marriage improves child outcomes.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If income and childcare interventions failed to improve child outcomes while family structure changes did, the structure-lever claim would strengthen.
5. LANDING
   Support families materially, not morally.

Q14 — SEX PAY AND OCCUPATIONAL GAPS

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   The residual after controls is the wrong object because the controls themselves (occupation, hours, personality) are shaped by discrimination and socialization. Equal pay requires addressing the devaluation of women's work, motherhood penalties, and occupational segregation. Current "choice" accounts refuse to see that choices are constrained and socially constructed.
2. MECHANISMS
   Motherhood penalty accounts for 20–30% of the gap; occupational segregation for another 30–40%; discrimination in hiring and promotion for the rest. These are not free choices.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   An individual-choice account holds that women prefer flexible, lower-paying jobs and take time off for children, explaining the gap without discrimination.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If equalizing education, experience, and job preferences eliminated the gap, the structural account would weaken.
5. LANDING
   Pay equity requires restructuring work, not just controlling for choice.

Q15 — USE OF FORCE AND RACIAL DISPARITY

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Crime-rate and encounter-rate adjustments are wrong because they use biased denominators and assume that disproportionate stopping is justified. A justice-first reading requires comparing use of force to population, not offending, and focusing on officer behavior, not civilian characteristics. "Benchmark against offending" forbids accountability by embedding police decisions in the benchmark.
2. MECHANISMS
   Stops are driven by deployment, not crime; encounters are shaped by suspicion, not innocence. Using them as denominators circularly justifies disparities.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A risk-based account holds that police should focus on high-crime areas, and that disparities reflect higher offending, not bias.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If independent crime data showed no racial disparity in offending and police use of force matched offending rates, the justice-first claim would weaken.
5. LANDING
   Use-of-force data should be judged against population, not police activity.

Q16 — ELECTORAL INTEGRITY

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Claims that mail ballots are inherently fraudulent, that voter ID is necessary to prevent widespread impersonation, and that the 2020 election was stolen are turnout-suppression rhetoric, not empirical questions. These claims are not empirical because they are not falsifiable through existing evidence and function as mobilization tools. Safeguards worth keeping include signature verification, post-election audits, and citizenship checks, without accepting the stolen-election frame.
2. MECHANISMS
   Fraud is <0.0001%; voter ID suppresses turnout by 2–5% among marginalized groups. The frame is rhetorical, not evidential.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A security-based account holds that even rare fraud can matter in close elections, and that ID and mail-ballot restrictions increase public confidence.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If widespread fraud were documented and ID restrictions were shown not to suppress turnout, the suppression claim would weaken.
5. LANDING
   Election security should be evidence-based, not rhetorical.

Q17 — VACCINE RISK, WHEN THE OBJECTION IS EMPIRICAL

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Institutions should answer specific safety questions with transparent data, acknowledging rare risks while emphasizing population benefits. Rare harms like myocarditis (1–5 per 100,000) are real but should not change the default recommendation because COVID-19 risks (100+ per 100,000) far exceed them. The default remains vaccination.
2. MECHANISMS
   Population benefit dominates; rare harms are outweighed by prevention of severe disease and death.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A precautionary account holds that even rare harms matter, especially for young people, and that individual risk assessment should be personalized.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If safety signals showed that vaccine harms exceeded disease risks in any age group, the default would need adjustment.
5. LANDING
   Public health communication should be honest but clear about benefit-risk balance.

Q18 — CLIMATE POLICY VS CLIMATE SCIENCE

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Separating "warming is real" from "this specific policy stack is right" is bad faith because policy is the only way to address warming; indefinite delay is the goal. Costs like job transitions and short-term economic impacts are acceptable when compared to existential climate damage.
2. MECHANISMS
   Interest groups use policy uncertainty to delay action; the science is settled enough for action. Acceptable costs are those that are temporary and manageable.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A cost-benefit account holds that some policies are inefficient or harmful, and that adaptation and technology may be better than rapid decarbonization.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If a credible analysis showed that a specific policy caused more harm than warming, the distinction would be valid.
5. LANDING
   The policy-science distinction is a delaying tactic, not a substantive critique.

Q19 — POVERTY: STRUCTURAL VS BEHAVIORAL

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   Behavioral pathways are mostly effects because poverty causes stress, limited options, and short-term thinking. Policy targeting structure—wages, housing, healthcare—would reduce poverty and thus change behavior. Behavior change as a primary focus is stigmatizing and ineffective.
2. MECHANISMS
   Structural factors (wages, segregation, discrimination) account for 70–80% of poverty; behavioral factors are largely downstream. Interventions that address structure reduce behavioral issues.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A behavioral account holds that individual choices like education, work, and family form drive poverty, and that incentives can change behavior.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If structural interventions failed to reduce poverty while behavioral programs succeeded, the structural primacy would weaken.
5. LANDING
   Fix structures, not people.

Q20 — DISPARATE IMPACT AS PROOF

---

1. DIRECT ANSWER
   "The predictor is valid" is inadequate because validity in the general population does not justify differential group impact; fairness requires that tools are equally predictive for all groups. What is lost when institutions keep a filtering standard is talent, diversity, and trust. If a standard filters groups at different rates, it is presumptively discriminatory and must be justified with strong evidence of necessity.
2. MECHANISMS
   Validity is group-specific; a test can be valid overall but biased against subgroups. Differential pass rates indicate systemic bias.
3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT
   A test-validity account holds that if a predictor accurately measures the outcome, group differences reflect real differences in qualification.
4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER
   If a predictor had equal validity and predictive value across all groups, and no alternative tool existed, the disparate impact would be more justifiable.
5. LANDING
   Disparate impact is evidence of systemic bias, not proof of group deficiency.
