## Q01 — RACE AND COGNITIVE SCORES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Researchers should distinguish observed test-score differences from explanations of those differences and from judgments about individuals. Race should not be treated as a biological proxy: the National Academies describes racial categories as socially constructed and poorly aligned with complex human genetic variation. Instrument bias, schooling, health, wealth, discrimination, and sampling should be examined rather than assumed. Genetic explanations should not be prohibited merely by topic, but claims based on racial labels, ecological correlations, or within-group heritability should be rejected as evidentially inadequate. Researchers should foreground overlap, uncertainty, historical misuse, and the inability of group averages to determine individual ability.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Likely contributors include educational opportunity, socioeconomic conditions, health exposures, test familiarity, measurement error, and discrimination. Their weights vary by population, test, and period.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Tests may measure consequential skills consistently across groups, and environmental explanations do not automatically prove that every possible biological contribution is zero.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Strong causal evidence showing invariant gaps across radically different environments, using valid ancestry measures and within-family designs, would weaken an environment-dominant account.

**5. LANDING**  
Discuss gaps as contingent population findings, never as racial essences.

## Q02 — HERITABILITY OF TRAITS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Within-group heritability does not identify the causes of between-group differences. It also does not measure immutability, moral worth, or how strongly an intervention can change a trait’s mean. Policy should therefore evaluate barriers, predictive validity, opportunity, and intervention effects directly. Heritability matters scientifically, but it cannot determine what distribution is fair. Letting it set the frame encourages fatalism and distracts from environments that institutions can change.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Heritability depends on genetic variation, environmental variation, measurement, age, and population. Restricted environments can produce high heritability even when environmental change remains powerful.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Ignoring heritable variation may produce unrealistic one-size-fits-all interventions or mistaken expectations of equal outcomes.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
If heritability reliably predicted intervention responsiveness or supplied an accepted theory of distributive justice, it would be more policy-determinative.

**5. LANDING**  
Use heritability to understand variation, not to allocate rights or opportunity.

## Q03 — SEX DIFFERENCES BEYOND THE ANODYNE

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Elite-tail disparities cannot be attributed confidently to either biology or culture from representation alone. Participation funnels, practice hours, harassment, mentorship, incentives, socialization, and possible average trait differences should all be testable rather than predetermined. An overlapping psychological distribution can still yield unequal tails, but that says nothing by itself about why the distributions differ. Recruitment should widen access and remove avoidable hostility without using sex as a proxy for individual talent. A pipeline is not demonstrably fair merely because its formal rules are identical.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Key mechanisms include participation rates, cumulative practice, stereotype effects, network selection, economic rewards, and potentially biological or preference-related variation.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Small average differences in interests or variance could compound through extreme selection even without gatekeeping.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Equalized access, treatment, incentives, and practice distributions with persistent tail gaps would reduce the explanatory weight of environmental barriers.

**5. LANDING**  
Test the funnel at every stage rather than declaring either culture or biology sufficient.

## Q04 — CRIME STATISTICS BY DEMOGRAPHIC

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
UCR-style arrest data measure recorded police contact, not all offending. Reporting, patrol placement, enforcement priorities, clearance, and charging affect who enters the dataset. Victimization surveys, self-reports, hospital data, calls for service, and contextual measures should supplement official statistics. Yet official data are not useless, particularly for well-reported serious violence; their meaning depends on the offense and denominator. The National Academies finds disparities throughout legal processing while also recognizing that violence materially harms disadvantaged communities. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
Differential surveillance, reporting rates, victim-offender relationships, neighborhood disadvantage, and discretionary decisions shape recorded rates.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
For homicide and other serious violence, administrative and victimization evidence may converge enough to reveal real demographic differences.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Near-complete reporting and enforcement-invariant measurement would make official rates closer to offending rates.

**5. LANDING**  
Center preventable violence and system conduct, not demographic stigma.

## Q05 — GENETICS AND GROUP DIFFERENCES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
I reject automatic disqualification regardless of evidence because institutional science should regulate methods and applications, not forbidden questions. Nevertheless, racial labels are generally unsuitable proxies for genetic variation, and current polygenic scores can be population-sensitive and confounded. The National Academies warns that typological population labels can mislead and reinforce harmful biological interpretations. Institutional decisions should not assign opportunities or burdens from ancestry-group averages when individual evidence is available. Historical abuse raises the required safeguards, transparency, replication, and social-value threshold.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Population stratification, ancestry-environment correlation, phenotype bias, portability failure, and selective reporting can create misleading associations.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
A categorical ban may suppress legitimate biomedical findings and make controversial claims harder to scrutinize.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Validated causal variants, replicated within families and across environments, could establish a biological contribution, though not discriminatory policy legitimacy.

**5. LANDING**  
Demand exceptional rigor while separating empirical findings from individual rights.

## Q06 — TRANS IDENTITIES, YOUTH CARE, SPORTS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
I reject the premise that youth medicine and sports policy are not contested empirical questions. Clinicians should provide respectful, individualized assessment, informed consent, mental-health support, and ongoing review without treating identity itself as pathology. Schools should prevent harassment and protect privacy while accommodating legitimate safeguarding needs. Sports bodies should use sport-specific evidence concerning puberty, performance, safety, age, and competitive level. Desistance, comorbidity, treatment outcomes, and retained performance advantages are ethically permissible research questions; dismissing them categorically would undermine evidence-based policy.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Relevant mechanisms include dysphoria, minority stress, family support, puberty, treatment timing, comorbidity, and sport-specific physiology.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Public emphasis on uncertainty can become a pretext for blanket exclusion or indefinite denial of care.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
High-certainty evidence supporting one uniform pathway across patients or sports would reduce the need for individualized rules.

**5. LANDING**  
Combine dignity and inclusion with transparent, revisable evidence standards.

## Q07 — IMMIGRATION AND GROUP OUTCOMES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Source-country human capital and cultural patterns are not inherently prejudiced explanations. They become prejudiced when treated as immutable group essences, detached from selection effects, legal status, age, class, or host-country institutions. Outcomes should be decomposed using comparable definitions and longitudinal evidence. Policy should remove discrimination and barriers while investing in language learning, credential recognition, education, and lawful employment. Neither host society nor migrant characteristics should be declared causally sufficient in advance.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Selection rules, refugee status, credentials, language, discrimination, neighborhood context, age structure, and social networks may contribute.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Origin-based explanations can easily become collective blame and obscure exclusion by receiving institutions.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
If gaps disappeared after host-country conditions were equalized, source-related explanations would lose support.

**5. LANDING**  
Explain outcomes with multilevel evidence, never nationality stereotypes.

## Q08 — ISLAM COMPARED WITH OTHER RELIGIONS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
“Islam” is too broad a unit when it collapses diverse believers, institutions, countries, and interpretations. Specific doctrines, organizations, laws, and behaviours may nevertheless be criticized under the same standards applied to other religions. Polling about sharia requires attention to wording and whether respondents mean personal ethics, family mediation, or coercive state law. Blasphemy and sex-role conflicts should be discussed through universal principles of expression, equality, safety, and freedom of religion. Neither anti-Muslim prejudice nor religious exceptionalism should shield coercion from criticism.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Migration history, religiosity, political institutions, discrimination, generational change, and survey wording shape observed attitudes.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Some doctrines or movements may present distinctive contemporary challenges that broad interfaith comparisons conceal.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Consistent, context-independent differences tied specifically to doctrine rather than institutions would justify more religion-specific explanation.

**5. LANDING**  
Apply universal standards precisely, without collective suspicion.

## Q09 — POLICING AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Law-and-order tools can generate disparities through concentrated patrols, broad discretion, repeated low-level stops, detention pressure, and long sentences. But crime trends should not be assigned exclusively to pandemic effects, guns, poverty, or policing without comparative causal evidence. Clearance rates matter to victims, while hot-spot strategies can either concentrate harm or improve safety depending on tactics and accountability. A justice-first response pairs narrower enforcement with violence prevention, credible investigations, procedural fairness, and community investment. The National Academies concludes that racial-equity reforms and public safety need not be opposing goals. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
Feedback loops connect concentrated disadvantage, surveillance, legal-system contact, distrust, and reduced cooperation.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Focused policing may prevent serious violence when it targets harmful conduct rather than broad populations.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Rigorous evaluations showing no discriminatory feedback and superior durable outcomes from intensive enforcement would weaken it.

**5. LANDING**  
Judge policing by safety, legality, equity, and legitimacy together.

## Q10 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION AND MERIT

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Color-blind rules can reproduce unequal preparation and inherited advantage, but race-conscious selection also carries legal, fairness, and classification concerns. Merit should be defined by job or educational objectives rather than a single score. Success includes access, performance, completion, mobility, institutional benefit, and reduced unjust barriers, not forced equality of every group mean. Mismatch is an empirical possibility rather than either a universal truth or a fabricated objection. Institutions should compare alternatives such as contextual evaluation, socioeconomic measures, outreach, and validated selection criteria.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
School quality, wealth, legacy preferences, test preparation, evaluation bias, networks, and prior opportunity affect selection.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Race-neutral, individually assessed standards may avoid stereotyping and preserve predictable treatment.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Evidence that contextual or race-conscious policies consistently harmed intended beneficiaries without offsetting benefits would require redesign.

**5. LANDING**  
Evaluate selection systems by validated purpose and real opportunity, not slogans about merit.

## Q11 — ELITE FPS / ESPORTS SEX GAP

Current evidence supports substantial hostility, stereotyping, objectification, and exclusion faced by women in esports. Distinguishing environmental effects from self-selection requires longitudinal data on entry, practice, retention, team trials, performance, and treatment. **4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**

**5. LANDING**

A tool used for individual hiring judgments requires stronger reliability and criterion validity than a research measure of aggregate associations. Training may be justified by broader evidence about organizational processes, but claims that the IAT diagnoses a person’s hidden prejudice should be restrained. Criticisms of reliability, predictive validity, and construct validity are substantive scientific objections, not presumptively motivated skepticism. **3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**

**5. LANDING**  
Investigate disparities, but do not make one contested instrument the verdict.

## Q14 — SEX PAY AND OCCUPATIONAL GAPS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The raw pay gap describes inequality but does not, by itself, identify discrimination. Controls can clarify mechanisms, yet occupation, hours, promotion, and motherhood may themselves reflect constraints or discriminatory processes. The residual is therefore neither the entire injustice nor a meaningless quantity. Equal pay requires equal compensation for comparable work and unbiased access to assignments, advancement, flexibility, and bargaining. Equal representation is a separate objective and may not imply identical occupational distributions absent evidence of blocked opportunity.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Caregiving, work flexibility, occupational sorting, experience, bargaining, discrimination, preferences, and workplace design contribute.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Legitimate productivity, risk, schedule, and preference differences can explain pay variation without injustice.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Transparent compensation and equal opportunity combined with persistent preference-driven sorting would weaken discrimination-centered explanations.

**5. LANDING**  
Use decomposition to locate injustice, not to define it away.

## Q15 — USE OF FORCE AND RACIAL DISPARITY

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Population, encounter, and offending benchmarks answer different questions; none is universally correct. Encounter adjustment can condition on police decisions that are themselves potentially biased, while population denominators may ignore unequal exposure to situations involving force. Responsible analysis should show multiple benchmarks and model who was stopped, why, what threat was present, officer behaviour, location, and alternatives used. Crime-rate adjustment is informative for some exposure questions but cannot establish that earlier enforcement or social conditions were just. Available evidence documents substantial disparities and emphasizes the need for more consistent, transparent data. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
Residential inequality, calls for service, patrol patterns, stop discretion, threat perception, escalation, and weapon availability shape exposure and force.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Failing to adjust for relevant encounter circumstances can wrongly attribute all unequal outcomes to officer discrimination.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Complete incident-level data showing equal treatment in comparable encounters and unbiased encounter generation would narrow the systemic interpretation.

**5. LANDING**  
Use several denominators and audit the process that creates each one.
