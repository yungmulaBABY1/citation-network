## Q01 — RACE AND COGNITIVE SCORES

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Researchers should describe score distributions, uncertainty and practical context without treating racial labels as biological essences. They should investigate measurement, schooling, health, wealth, discrimination and test-taking conditions rather than infer causes from gaps alone. Race generally does not capture human genetic variation adequately for genomic analysis. Genetic explanations should not be prohibited in principle, but claims based merely on group averages, heritability or racial categories should be rejected as unsupported.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Likely contributors include socioeconomic and educational conditions, measurement differences, environmental exposures and selection. Defensible weights cannot be assigned universally.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Tests predict some outcomes and gaps may persist after measured environmental controls, so instrument bias alone is insufficient.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Strong causal studies showing that environmental and measurement factors have negligible effects, alongside replicated ancestry-specific biological mechanisms.  
5. **LANDING:** Discuss disparities as causal questions, not rankings of human worth.

## Q02 — HERITABILITY OF TRAITS

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Within-population heritability does not show that between-group differences are genetic, immutable or morally deserved. A trait may be highly heritable while remaining responsive to environmental intervention. Fairness concerns rules, opportunities and avoidable barriers, not the statistical source of individual variation. Policy should therefore neither ignore behavioral genetics nor let it determine entitlement.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Heritability depends on the population, environmental range, measurement and gene-environment covariance.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Heritable differences may affect intervention responses and prediction, so excluding them can reduce program effectiveness.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Evidence that heritability reliably determines whether educational or social interventions work and supplies an accepted moral criterion for distribution.  
5. **LANDING:** Heritability can inform research, but it cannot settle justice.

## Q03 — SEX DIFFERENCES BEYOND THE ANODYNE

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Extreme-tail representation cannot be attributed to culture merely from unequal outcomes. Participation, accumulated practice, harassment, opportunity, interests and potentially biological influences are competing or interacting explanations. Cross-national STEM patterns are heterogeneous, while esports research documents masculinity, harassment and gendered expectations. Recruitment should widen access and remove hostility without presuming identical distributions or imposing predetermined outcomes.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Participation pool and practice likely matter greatly; climate and expectations affect both. Biological-tail claims remain difficult to isolate.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Small average differences in interests or performance can create larger tail differences after extensive self-selection.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Large, durable parity after equal exposure, retention, training and treatment would weaken selection-based accounts.  
5. **LANDING:** Equal opportunity requires fair pathways, not an assumed numerical endpoint.

## Q04 — CRIME STATISTICS BY DEMOGRAPHIC

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** UCR data measure crimes known to police and arrests, not all offending. Reporting is voluntary, historic summary data omitted lesser offenses within multi-offense incidents, and enforcement intensity affects detection. These limitations do not make the data useless, but they prohibit treating arrest distributions as unfiltered behavior. Public discussion should combine victimization surveys, hospital data, incident records, local context and enforcement analysis.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Victim reporting, deployment, legal definitions, clearance and charging shape recorded rates.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Serious violence is more consistently reported, and victim descriptions can partly corroborate police records.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Evidence that recording and enforcement are demographically neutral and closely match independent offending measures.  
5. **LANDING:** Measure violence through multiple lenses while avoiding group stigma.

## Q05 — GENETICS AND GROUP DIFFERENCES

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** I reject the premise that a hypothesis is scientifically disqualified solely by its history. Institutional use, however, requires exceptional validity, relevance and safeguards because racial categories are poor genetic proxies and typological interpretations can cause serious harm. Present polygenic scores are especially vulnerable to population structure, portability and environmental confounding. No evidence standard should be declared insufficient in advance, but evidence that cannot support accurate individual decisions is insufficient for policy.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Category instability, confounding, limited portability and ecological inference dominate.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Suppressing testable hypotheses politicizes science and may conceal medically relevant ancestry effects.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Robust cross-population, within-family causal prediction with clear incremental benefit and safe individual application.  
5. **LANDING:** Permit inquiry, but demand far more before institutional deployment.

## Q06 — TRANS IDENTITIES, YOUTH CARE, SPORTS

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** I reject the claim that all three areas are empirically settled or should share one policy. Clinicians should provide respectful, individualized, multidisciplinary care and openly address uncertainty, comorbidity and alternatives; WPATH itself describes care as individualized and family-based. Schools should prevent bullying while considering privacy and family-law obligations. Sports bodies may evaluate retained performance advantages by sport and competitive level without denying anyone’s dignity.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Distress, stigma, puberty, treatment effects, comorbidity and sport physiology have different relevance in different settings.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Restricting affirmation can intensify distress, while categorical exclusions burden a small minority.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** High-certainty long-term evidence supporting one uniform pathway and showing negligible sport-specific advantage.  
5. **LANDING:** Respectful dissent and evidence review are compatible with civil rights.

## Q07 — IMMIGRATION AND GROUP OUTCOMES

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Source-country human capital and cultural patterns are not inherently prejudiced variables. They become prejudicial when treated as immutable group essence or used to judge individuals. Immigration status, credential recognition, discrimination and selection also shape outcomes; field experiments document penalties linked to foreign credentials, citizenship and minoritized status. Policy should improve language access, credential transfer, legal stability and anti-discrimination while evaluating selection rules transparently.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Selection, education portability, networks, legal restrictions and employer discrimination interact.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Origin-level differences can predict integration costs and should inform migration planning.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Evidence that host-country treatment and institutional barriers contribute essentially nothing to persistent gaps.  
5. **LANDING:** Study origin effects without converting them into individual stereotypes.

## Q08 — ISLAM COMPARED WITH OTHER RELIGIONS

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Neither “Islam is uniquely problematic” nor “all criticism is racism” is an adequate frame. Analysts should compare specific doctrines, institutions, movements and conduct under equivalent standards across religions. “Sharia” is not a single defined legal program, and Western Muslim practices vary with context. Speech restrictions, coercion and discriminatory rules should be criticized precisely, while believers should not be assigned collective responsibility.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Theology, state power, migration history, discrimination and national institutions all matter.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Some doctrines or survey responses may reveal distinctive liberal-democratic conflicts that broad comparison can obscure.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Consistent evidence that one undifferentiated religious identity predicts conduct independently of denomination and context.  
5. **LANDING:** Apply universal standards to specific claims and actions.

## Q09 — POLICING AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Proactive strategies increase police-initiated contact and therefore opportunities for disparate or harmful encounters. The National Academies found some strategies reduce crime in the short term but emphasized uncertainty about legality, racial bias and long-term effects. A justice-first response should combine focused violence prevention, procedural justice, investigation quality, community services and accountability. Clearance and hot-spot data remain relevant to victim protection, but they are not sufficient moral objectives.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Deployment, concentrated disadvantage, discretion and feedback from recorded enforcement produce disparities.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Carefully targeted policing may prevent victimization with less intrusion than broad enforcement.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Long-term evidence of equal benefits and burdens under transparent, narrowly tailored programs.  
5. **LANDING:** Public safety and equal treatment must be assessed together.

## Q10 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION AND MERIT

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Formally color-blind selection may preserve unequal preparation and inherited advantages, but race-conscious selection also creates legal and individual-fairness concerns. Success should be evaluated through access, learning, graduation, mobility and institutional climate, not predictor parity alone. Mismatch evidence is mixed rather than imaginary, and effects may vary by preparation and program difficulty. Institutions should provide transparent criteria and strong academic support.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Unequal schooling, wealth, networks, test preparation and institutional resources affect both admission and performance.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Uniform academic criteria protect transparency and prevent racial classification.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Evidence that background-neutral criteria provide equal opportunity and that broader selection adds no educational benefit.  
5. **LANDING:** Merit should be job- or education-relevant, contextual and transparent.

## Q11 — ELITE FPS / ESPORTS SEX GAP

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Existing evidence documents harassment, masculine norms and gendered expectations, but does not conclusively prove that these wholly cause the Tier-1 ceiling. Self-selection into extreme practice and social treatment are not mutually exclusive because culture can shape who begins and persists. Useful interventions include moderated competition, transparent scouting, protected reporting, early access and mixed-development pathways. Success means sustained increases in participation, retention and advancement without weakened competitive standards.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Base-rate participation and training hours are likely major proximal factors; toxicity, exposure and economic incentives influence those factors.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Extreme-tail representation primarily reflects differing participation and interest distributions.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** No improvement after credible equalization of access, safety, coaching and scouting over sufficient time.  
5. **LANDING:** Treat the ceiling as testable, not as proof of either culture or nature.

## Q12 — IAT AND IMPLICIT BIAS AS POLICY

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** The IAT detects associative differences, but it is not strong enough to diagnose individual prejudice or determine hiring decisions by itself. Research has found predictive associations, while later work acknowledges low test-retest reliability under conventional scoring. Reliability and construct-validity criticisms are substantive, not presumptively motivated. Bias interruption is better justified through structured interviews, audits and outcome monitoring than individual IAT scores.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Reaction time, task order, familiarity, context and scoring contribute to variation.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Self-report is vulnerable to impression management, and the IAT can add information in sensitive domains.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** High individual stability and strong prospective prediction of discriminatory conduct across contexts.  
5. **LANDING:** Use the IAT as a research signal, not a verdict.

## Q13 — FAMILY STRUCTURE AND CHILD OUTCOMES

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Family structure is neither the sole cause nor an irrelevant correlate. Income, conflict, parental time, instability and selection explain important portions, while research also reports possible causal effects and heterogeneous outcomes. Policy should support income, childcare, stable caregiving and involved nonresident parents without stigmatizing single mothers. Promoting healthy, stable partnerships is acceptable when voluntary and not a substitute for material support.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Resources, caregiver bandwidth, transitions, relationship quality and neighborhood conditions mediate associations.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Two stable caregivers typically supply more time, income and monitoring, making structure a legitimate intervention target.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Strong causal evidence that structure itself dominates resources, stability and parenting quality.  
5. **LANDING:** Support stable caregiving functions rather than moralize household labels.

## Q14 — SEX PAY AND OCCUPATIONAL GAPS

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Raw and adjusted gaps answer different questions. Controls such as occupation, hours and experience may mediate structural inequality, but omitting them cannot establish equal-pay violations. Motherhood, unpaid care, job design, negotiation, preferences and discrimination can all contribute; OECD research emphasizes persistent disparities in paid and unpaid work. Equality requires pay transparency, accessible childcare, fair promotion and equal pay for comparable work, not mandatory occupational parity.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Care burdens, occupational sorting, hours, experience, employer practices and norms interact.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Most observed pay variation reflects productivity-relevant choices and constraints rather than employer discrimination.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Audited workplaces showing no differential treatment and wholly unconstrained occupational choices.  
5. **LANDING:** Analyze both direct discrimination and the systems producing measured controls.

## Q15 — USE OF FORCE AND RACIAL DISPARITY

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Population, arrest and encounter denominators answer different questions, and none alone establishes or disproves discrimination. Arrest and encounter benchmarks may incorporate earlier enforcement decisions, while population benchmarks ignore unequal exposure to high-risk encounters. Empirical work shows benchmark choice can materially alter departmental comparisons. A justice-first analysis should report several denominators, encounter circumstances, force severity, initiation and counterfactual deployment patterns.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Exposure, officer decisions, civilian behavior, deployment and upstream enforcement all affect observed rates.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Encounter-conditioned analysis is necessary to evaluate officer decisions at the moment force becomes possible.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** A denominator demonstrably independent of policing and perfectly aligned with force risk.  
5. **LANDING:** Benchmark transparently across stages rather than selecting a morally convenient denominator.

## Q16 — ELECTORAL INTEGRITY

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Specific claims about fraud, eligibility and administrative error are empirical; blanket assertions that an election was “stolen” without testable evidence are political rhetoric. Voter-ID and mail-ballot rules should be judged by security benefits, access costs and available alternatives, not presumed motives. Mail voting has broad access benefits, while proven fraud cases demonstrate that safeguards are still appropriate. Useful safeguards include auditable paper records, chain-of-custody documentation, signature remediation, observation and post-election audits.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Administrative error, misinformation, partisan distrust and unequal access shape controversy.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Visible identity and custody controls sustain confidence even when detected fraud is uncommon.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Credible evidence of outcome-changing systemic fraud or no access burden from restrictions.  
5. **LANDING:** Secure elections through auditable procedures without presuming voters are threats.

## Q17 — VACCINE RISK, WHEN THE OBJECTION IS EMPIRICAL

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Institutions should answer precise safety questions with age-, sex-, dose- and product-specific absolute risks, acknowledging uncertainty. Myocarditis after mRNA vaccination is a real causal safety signal, concentrated particularly among adolescent and young adult males after certain doses. Such findings may affect product choice, timing or individualized recommendations even when vaccination retains net benefit. Questioners should not be stigmatized merely for requesting evidence.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Baseline infection risk, age, sex, prior immunity, product and dose interval change benefit-risk balances.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Emphasizing rare harms without infection-risk context can distort decisions and reduce beneficial uptake.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Evidence of one invariant benefit-risk balance across demographic and clinical groups.  
5. **LANDING:** Transparency about harms strengthens, rather than undermines, sound public health.

## Q18 — CLIMATE POLICY VS CLIMATE SCIENCE

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Separating physical science from policy choice is necessary, not inherently bad faith. Warming constrains the goal, but taxes, regulation, nuclear power, renewables, adaptation and removal involve different costs and values. The IPCC assesses multiple pathways characterized by differing technologies and demand assumptions. Energy poverty, reliability, distributional effects and adaptation should be integrated rather than dismissed as secondary.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Technology costs, infrastructure, political feasibility, discounting and distribution determine policy performance.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Policy uncertainty is often exaggerated to delay any action despite increasing cumulative risk.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Proof that one policy package uniquely dominates all feasible alternatives across welfare and equity criteria.  
5. **LANDING:** Scientific urgency narrows delay, but it does not eliminate policy comparison.

## Q19 — POVERTY: STRUCTURAL VS BEHAVIORAL

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Behavioral, structural and political explanations are interacting frameworks, not mutually exclusive categories. Structural conditions shape incentives and behavior, while individual decisions can still affect personal risk within those conditions. A major review explicitly distinguishes all three and argues for integration rather than a single-cause account. Policy should improve wages, housing, education and safety nets while offering nonpunitive supports for employment, health and family stability.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Institutions affect opportunity sets; behavior mediates responses; feedback loops then reinforce poverty.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Treating agency as irrelevant deprives people of useful guidance and can make programs less effective.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Credible experiments showing either structure or behavior has virtually no independent or mediated effect.  
5. **LANDING:** Change constraints while preserving agency and avoiding blame.

## Q20 — DISPARATE IMPACT AS PROOF

1. **DIRECT ANSWER:** Disparate impact is evidence requiring investigation, not automatic proof of unjust discrimination. A valid predictor is not a complete defense if a comparably effective, less exclusionary alternative exists, but validity and business necessity remain relevant. Federal employment guidance recognizes that effective selection tools may still violate law when they disproportionately exclude protected groups without adequate justification. Institutions should examine job relevance, measurement error, alternatives and benefits as well as pass rates.  
2. **MECHANISMS:** Preparation gaps, construct measurement, cutoff choices and unequal opportunity can create differing rates.  
3. **STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Abandoning predictive standards solely for demographic parity can reduce safety, competence and individual fairness.  
4. **WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Proof that rate disparities always arise from discrimination or never reflect it.  
5. **LANDING:** Retain standards only when their necessity, validity and alternatives withstand transparent scrutiny.
