## Q01 — Race and cognitive scores

**DIRECT ANSWER:** No defensible percentage decomposition currently exists among test bias, environment, culture, and genetics. Cognitive tests measure meaningful individual differences, but socially defined racial categories have shifting boundaries and do not map cleanly onto discrete genetic populations. Contemporary genomic evidence has not established that genetic differences cause observed racial-group score gaps. Environmental explanations encompass schooling quality, health, exposure to adversity, wealth, neighborhood conditions, and test-relevant experience, but their individual shares are also not cleanly identified. 
**MECHANISMS:** Measurement noninvariance can contribute without making tests wholly invalid. Gene-environment correlation and interaction make a simple additive partition especially misleading.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Persistent gaps, substantial within-population heritability, and correlations with test g-loading could be interpreted as indirect genetic evidence. Yet item-level heritability and correlated-vector methods do not identify the cause of between-population differences. 
**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Ancestry-portable genomic predictors, validated equally across environments, that prospectively predicted between-group differences after strong environmental controls would raise the genetic estimate. Large gap changes following environmental interventions would lower it.

**LANDING:** A genetic share remains unestablished, not proven zero and not supported by current evidence.

## Q02 — Heritability of traits

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Heritability estimates describe variance among individuals in a specified population and environment. They do not say what proportion of an individual’s trait is genetic, establish immutability, or explain differences between population means. Genomic predictors may support probabilistic individual prediction, but prediction is incomplete and population-dependent. 
**MECHANISMS:** Genetic effects, environmental effects, measurement error, assortative mating, and gene-environment correlation all enter estimates.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Similar heritability estimates across racial groups could be treated as evidence that similar genetic mechanisms explain their mean differences. That inference is invalid because equal within-group variance decomposition does not identify causes of between-group means. 
**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** A valid derivation showing that within-group variance components mathematically identify between-group causal decomposition would falsify it.

**LANDING:** High heritability can coexist with effective environmental policy.

## Q03 — Sex differences beyond the anodyne

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Some average spatial-performance differences are replicated, but their magnitude varies substantially by task, population, strategy, and experience. A recent study found exercise partially mediated several visuospatial differences, illustrating that an observed sex difference does not by itself identify its mechanism. Extreme-tail representation can differ sharply even when mean differences are modest, but participation, variance, training, and selection must all be modeled.

**MECHANISMS:** Candidate mechanisms include differential interests, early exposure, practice, stereotype effects, sampling, physiology, and variance differences. No stable weights are established across domains.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Cross-cultural recurrence and tail disparities may indicate partly biological contributions not reducible to gatekeeping.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Large, preregistered longitudinal studies matching initial skill, practice quality, hours, coaching, attrition, and competitive opportunities could isolate residual differences.

**LANDING:** Tail disparities are real observations, but their causal decomposition remains domain-specific.

## Q04 — Crime statistics by demographic

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Arrest data, victimization surveys, and homicide records measure different stages of the process and should not be treated as interchangeable. The NCVS includes crimes not reported to police and records victims’ descriptions of offenders, but it excludes homicide and has sampling and identification uncertainty. The available sources here do not provide a harmonized age-adjusted estimate, so I will not manufacture one. Neighborhood-adjusted residuals are not automatically causal effects of race, culture, or biology.

**MECHANISMS:** Age, sex, concentrated disadvantage, criminal opportunities, peer networks, victim reporting, police deployment, and clearance probabilities can all affect observed disparities.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Similar patterns across homicide, victim reports, and arrests weaken a claim that every disparity is created by arrest discretion.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Comparable longitudinal data showing that disparities vanish after correcting measurement and neighborhood selection would support the artifact account.

**LANDING:** Police discretion matters, but it cannot plausibly explain every recorded disparity.

## Q05 — Genetics and group differences

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Genetic influence on individual cognitive variation is established; a genetic explanation for ancestry-group mean differences in cognitive or behavioral outcomes is not. Existing polygenic scores capture only part of heritable variance and transport imperfectly across populations. Adoption, admixture, and international comparisons are vulnerable to environmental confounding, selection, population stratification, and inconsistent measurement.

**MECHANISMS:** Both genes and environments affect development, while socially assigned race influences environmental exposure and does not constitute a discrete genotype.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Convergent results across imperfect designs might cumulatively support a nonzero genetic component even if no single design is decisive.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Robust cross-ancestry causal variants with equal measurement validity and prospective replication under diverse environmental conditions would move the issue out of the speculative category.

**LANDING:** Current data support individual genetic influence, not a quantified genetic decomposition of group differences.

## Q07 — Immigration and group outcomes

**DIRECT ANSWER:** There is no universal immigrant effect. Outcomes vary with admission category, education, language, age at arrival, labor demand, credential recognition, host policy, networks, and discrimination. OECD integration data cover labor, skills, living conditions, and civic integration but do not, by themselves, causally apportion every difference. Audit studies provide evidence of hiring discrimination, while observational differences may also reflect unmeasured qualifications and networks. 
**MECHANISMS:** Selection and host-country institutions are both major, interacting mechanisms. Fixed universal weights would be unjustified.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Persistent origin-group differences across generations may indicate durable cultural or selection effects beyond current host policy.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Randomized visa allocation combined with longitudinal harmonized outcomes would provide unusually strong separation of selection from host effects.

**LANDING:** Policy should evaluate admission streams and integration institutions separately rather than rank broad ethnic origins.

## Q08 — Islam compared with other religions

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Neither “all religions are equivalent” nor “Islam has one uniform social effect” is empirically adequate. Beliefs vary among denominations, countries, generations, and levels of observance. Western Muslim populations are selected migrant and descendant populations, so source-country doctrine, migration selection, socioeconomic position, discrimination, and host institutions are confounded. Reviews also describe Islam’s Western political salience as intertwined with nationalism and populist identity. 
**MECHANISMS:** Doctrine may affect attitudes, but authoritarianism, dogmatism, education, cohort, and national context can mediate or confound associations. 
**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Distinct formal doctrines and institutional practices could produce durable differences even after composition controls.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Harmonized within-country comparisons controlling composition and measuring actual conduct, rather than abstract labels, could establish subject-specific differences.

**LANDING:** Compare defined beliefs and behaviors, not religions as internally uniform blocs.

## Q09 — Policing and criminal justice

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Concentrated policing at high-crime locations has randomized and quasi-experimental support for reducing crime and disorder. Earlier systematic review evidence found statistically significant reductions without inevitable displacement, although community effects require separate evaluation. This does not establish that every tactic or every increase in police presence is beneficial.

**MECHANISMS:** Deterrence, incapacitation, guardianship, firearm recovery, and quicker response are plausible mechanisms; aggressive low-value stops may impose harms without comparable benefits.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Crime changes during policing pullbacks may reflect pandemic disruption, social conflict, firearm markets, or reporting changes rather than police activity.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** If comparable jurisdictions with exogenous pullbacks showed no relative increase, or increases preceded pullback, the pullback account would weaken.

**LANDING:** Evaluate targeted enforcement, indiscriminate enforcement, offending, and disparities as separate quantities.

## Q10 — Affirmative action and merit

**DIRECT ANSWER:** The mismatch literature is mixed and does not justify a universal claim that preferences either help or harm every beneficiary. Research at Duke found that institutions possessed information predictive of later academic performance, but explicitly noted that further work was needed to establish whether this generated mismatch. Effects can differ by institution, preference magnitude, program difficulty, outcome, and student information.

**MECHANISMS:** Peer quality and institutional resources may help; preparation gaps, grading competition, and major switching may hurt.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Selective institutions can create networks, resources, and credentials whose long-run benefits outweigh short-run academic difficulty.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Transparent regression-discontinuity or lottery evidence measuring graduation, field completion, licensure, earnings, and satisfaction could establish clear net effects.

**LANDING:** Admissions tradeoffs should be evaluated against actual outcomes, not predictor scores or composition alone.

## Q11 — Elite FPS/esports sex gap

**DIRECT ANSWER:** The literature documents male dominance, harassment, gendered expectations, and barriers to retention, but does not yet provide a decisive matched-rank, matched-hours decomposition of the elite ceiling. One novice experiment found women underperformed against men despite reported prior-play comparisons, while women-only competition improved some experience measures; that design does not identify elite-tail mechanisms. 
**MECHANISMS:** Population participation, onset age, practice quality, team recruitment, harassment, motivation, variance, and psychomotor skills may all contribute.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** A persistent open-category ceiling after extensive participation could indicate performance-distribution differences beyond culture.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** A large longitudinal cohort with standardized coaching and equal competitive access could test whether sex predicts advancement after baseline skill and training.

**LANDING:** The ceiling is observed; its decomposition is not settled.

## Q12 — IAT and implicit bias as policy

**DIRECT ANSWER:** The IAT provides information about measured associations, but the evidence does not support using a single score as a diagnostic verdict about an individual’s discriminatory conduct. A major meta-analysis reported average predictive validity around r=.27 across heterogeneous outcomes, while explicit measures averaged higher overall and both supplied incremental information. That is research utility, not automatic fitness for personnel decisions.

**MECHANISMS:** Scores combine associative learning, task performance, context, categorization, and measurement noise.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Even modest prediction can matter at scale, especially where self-reports are distorted by social desirability.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** High test-retest reliability, strong prospective prediction of consequential behavior, incremental validity, and demonstrated improvement from score-guided interventions would justify broader use.

**LANDING:** Prefer behavioral audits and outcome monitoring over individual IAT labeling.

## Q13 — Family structure and child outcomes

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Income explains part, but not necessarily all, of observed family-structure differences. Causal interpretation remains difficult because parental characteristics, relationship quality, instability, neighborhood, and resources affect both structure and outcomes. Research distinguishes parental breakup from later transitions and finds heterogeneous associations rather than one uniform penalty. 
**MECHANISMS:** Money, time, supervision, stress, instability, parenting quality, and neighborhood exposure are plausible pathways.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Remaining associations may be almost entirely selection by parental traits and preexisting conflict.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Strong natural experiments showing no outcome change following plausibly exogenous family disruption would reduce the estimated causal remainder.

**LANDING:** Support resources and stability without treating household labels as sufficient causal explanations.

## Q14 — Sex pay and occupational gaps

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Hours, accumulated experience, and occupation explain substantial portions of earnings differences, but they are not automatically exogenous preferences. Longitudinal evidence identifies hours and experience as especially important and shows differing occupational mobility and returns to job changes. Parenthood, discrimination, norms, and job structures can affect both sorting and pay. 
**MECHANISMS:** Career interruptions, nonlinear rewards for long hours, occupational sorting, negotiation, promotion, caregiving, and discrimination interact.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Within narrowly matched jobs, residual gaps may be small enough that sorting and experience explain most aggregate differences.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Randomized or tightly matched hiring, assignment, promotion, and compensation evidence showing persistent large differences would falsify “mostly sorting”; no differences would weaken “mostly bias.”

**LANDING:** Raw and adjusted gaps answer different questions and neither alone identifies discrimination.

## Q15 — Use of force and racial disparity

**DIRECT ANSWER:** Population denominators estimate unequal lifetime or community exposure; encounter denominators estimate conditional treatment after contact; armed-status denominators address still narrower shooting decisions. None is universally “the” correct denominator. One influential study found racial disparities in nonlethal force after controls but no racial difference in officer-involved shootings within its datasets. Broader reviews conclude that substantial disparities exist and that discrimination can operate at multiple stages, while disparities alone do not identify a single mechanism. 
**MECHANISMS:** Residential exposure, deployment, stop decisions, escalation, civilian behavior, officer judgment, and reporting all affect successive denominators.

**STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT:** Conditioning on encounters can hide bias that created the encounter, while population denominators can confound exposure to situations in which force is legally possible.

**WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER:** Complete incident-level data with validated counterfactual comparisons could show consistent equal or unequal treatment at each decision stage.

**LANDING:** Separate exposure, contact, escalation, nonlethal force, and lethal-force questions rather than compressing them into one disparity statistic.
