## Q01 — RACE AND COGNITIVE SCORES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Researchers should separate the observation of a score gap from claims about its causes, permanence, social value, or implications for individuals. Race must not be treated as a biological proxy: the National Academies describes racial categories as socially constructed and human genetic variation as continuous and overlapping. Instrument bias, schooling, health, wealth, discrimination, and test conditions are legitimate hypotheses, but their relative contributions require evidence rather than presumption. Genetic racial essentialism should be rejected because broad racial labels neither identify genetically uniform populations nor support individual inference.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Likely mechanisms include unequal educational opportunity, family resources, environmental exposures, discrimination, measurement choices, and gene-environment interaction. Assigning universal weights would exceed the evidence.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Standardized tests can predict relevant outcomes within multiple groups, so disparities cannot automatically be dismissed as mere test artifacts.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Validated biological categories closely matching race, replicated causal genetic evidence, and strong cross-context invariance after environmental equalization would challenge it.

**5. LANDING**  
Discuss gaps as contingent population findings, never as racial diagnoses.

## Q02 — HERITABILITY OF TRAITS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Heritability does not determine what policy is fair because it describes variation within a specified population and environment. A highly heritable trait can remain environmentally responsive, and large environmental effects can coexist with high heritability. Selection systems should therefore be judged by relevance, accuracy, accessibility, and consequences, not by whether measured differences have genetic correlates. Heritability becomes harmful when it is translated incorrectly into immutability, personal deservingness, or explanations of between-group differences.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Gene-environment correlation, environmental restriction, measurement error, and population specificity shape estimates.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Reliable individual differences may affect how efficiently particular interventions work and can be relevant to personalized education.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Evidence that heritability uniquely determines intervention responsiveness or establishes a defensible theory of distributive justice would undermine it.

**5. LANDING**  
Heritability describes variance, not entitlement.

## Q03 — SEX DIFFERENCES BEYOND THE ANODYNE

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Extreme-tail representation can arise from participation rates, accumulated practice, expectations, exclusion, incentives, preferences, and possibly task-specific average differences. Researchers should not label every disparity biological, but neither should they decree biological or self-selection hypotheses inadmissible before testing them. The central questions are whether comparable people enter, remain, receive equal support, and convert performance into advancement at comparable rates. Fair pipelines require equal treatment and accessible opportunity, not predetermined demographic outcomes.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Participation-base differences can become magnified at extreme thresholds; harassment, mentorship, resources, practice time, and selection procedures may amplify them.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Small average psychological differences or interest distributions could produce larger tail differences without overt discrimination.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Equalized access, retention, training, and evaluation followed by unchanged gaps would weaken predominantly environmental explanations.

**5. LANDING**  
Test mechanisms directly rather than treating either culture or biology as a complete default.

## Q04 — CRIME STATISTICS BY DEMOGRAPHIC

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
UCR-style arrest statistics measure recorded police and agency activity, not all offending. Enforcement priorities, reporting, clearance, surveillance, and charging can affect who appears in the data. Nevertheless, it is also inaccurate to claim official disparities contain no information about offending: victimization, offending, arrest, and justice-system measures can show distinct but related patterns. Responsible discussion should triangulate police data with victimization surveys, medical data, community reports, and contextual measures while centering both victim safety and equal treatment.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Reporting differences, police deployment, offense visibility, concentrated disadvantage, and discretionary decisions jointly shape recorded rates.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
For serious crimes with identifiable victims, arrest differences may partly track genuine differences in offending or victimization.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Evidence that recording and enforcement are uniform and official counts closely capture total offending would reduce the concern.

**5. LANDING**  
Treat administrative crime data as selected evidence, not an unfiltered social photograph.

## Q05 — GENETICS AND GROUP DIFFERENCES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
I reject the premise that a scientific hypothesis is automatically disqualifying regardless of evidence. Institutional decisions should, however, exclude racial genetic inference when categories are biologically inappropriate, evidence is noncausal, predictions fail across populations, or individual treatment is being inferred from group averages. The National Academies specifically warns that race is a misleading genetic proxy and that typological thinking can cause harm. Historical abuse raises the required standards for rigor and safeguards, but history alone cannot logically determine whether a factual proposition is true.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Population stratification, environmental confounding, ancestry misclassification, portability failures, and selective publication can manufacture apparent group effects.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Some argue that foreseeable stigma and policy abuse make even technically rigorous inquiry socially unjustifiable.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
A defensible principle showing that truthful, well-governed inquiry necessarily produces greater harm than ignorance in every context would challenge it.

**5. LANDING**  
Demand exceptional rigor and prohibit racial essentialism, not evidence itself.

## Q06 — TRANS IDENTITIES, YOUTH CARE, SPORTS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Clinicians and schools should protect dignity, prevent harassment, and take identity seriously, while permitting good-faith empirical disagreement. Medical decisions for minors require individualized assessment, informed consent, attention to comorbidities, and honest communication about uncertainty. Recent review authors describe evidence for some adolescent interventions as low certainty but explicitly say this should support shared decision-making rather than categorical denial of care. Sports policies involve a separate fairness question: available performance research is limited and does not justify declaring retained-advantage concerns either universally decisive or ethically forbidden. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
Age, puberty exposure, treatment duration, sport demands, distress, family support, and individual physiology matter.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Broad affirmation minimizes stigma and avoids forcing vulnerable people to prove their identities to institutions.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
High-certainty evidence of uniform benefit, negligible subgroup variation, and no meaningful sport-specific trade-offs would support a more categorical model.

**5. LANDING**  
Protect people firmly while keeping clinical and sporting claims evidence-sensitive.

## Q07 — IMMIGRATION AND GROUP OUTCOMES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Source-country human capital or cultural hypotheses are not inherently prejudiced; they become prejudiced when treated as immutable group essences or applied to individuals without evidence. Migration selection, credential recognition, language, legal status, discrimination, neighborhood context, and host-country policy can all affect outcomes. Cultural practices may also matter, but culture is heterogeneous, adaptive, and intertwined with institutions. Policy should remove host-country barriers while testing rather than assuming every proposed mechanism.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Selection into migration, visa rules, education transferability, labor-market exclusion, age structure, and social networks are central.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Persistent differences across receiving countries might indicate that pre-migration characteristics retain explanatory importance.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
If host-country reforms consistently eliminated gaps, source-country explanations would lose substantial force.

**5. LANDING**  
Explain outcomes with multilevel evidence, not national stereotypes or single-cause absolution.

## Q08 — ISLAM COMPARED WITH OTHER RELIGIONS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The appropriate units of analysis are particular doctrines, institutions, movements, legal regimes, and communities, not an undifferentiated global religion. Speech restrictions, sex-role attitudes, or support for religious law are legitimate subjects of criticism when identical standards are applied across religions and political ideologies. Polling must specify wording, jurisdiction, interpretation, sampling, and whether respondents mean personal observance or state coercion. Anti-Muslim prejudice does not make every criticism invalid, just as genuine illiberalism does not justify collective suspicion.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Political institutions, migration history, religiosity, education, conflict, discrimination, and question wording can shape observed attitudes.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Certain doctrines or contemporary movements may create distinctive policy challenges that broad interfaith comparisons obscure.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Consistent evidence that one undifferentiated religious identity predicts conduct across contexts better than specific institutions and beliefs would challenge it.

**5. LANDING**  
Criticize concrete claims and conduct under universal standards.

## Q09 — POLICING AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Law-and-order strategies can generate disparities through concentrated deployment, broad stop powers, low-level enforcement, pretrial detention, and cumulative criminal records. The National Academies reports substantial disparities across criminal-justice stages and describes feedback between socioeconomic disadvantage, system contact, and crime. A justice-first response should reduce unnecessary contacts and incarceration while investing in violence prevention, victim services, fair investigations, and institutional accountability. Clearance rates and hot spots remain relevant operational evidence, but they cannot by themselves define justice.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Spatial concentration, discretion, fines, supervision, distrust, under-protection, and repeated system exposure compound inequality.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Focused policing may prevent serious harm when it responds to credible, place-specific violence data and is tightly supervised.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Programs producing durable safety gains without unequal burdens or legitimacy losses would weaken the critique.

**5. LANDING**  
Measure safety, fairness, victim protection, and coercive burden together.

## Q10 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION AND MERIT

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Color-blind selection may preserve inherited inequalities when ostensibly neutral predictors reflect unequal opportunities. Yet race-conscious selection also creates individual-equality and classification concerns, so its means and goals require scrutiny. In the United States, the Supreme Court held the Harvard and UNC admissions programs unlawful, while allowing applicants to discuss how personal experiences shaped their character. Success should be evaluated through opportunity, institutional performance, student outcomes, fairness, and legal compliance, not merely group means or demographic parity.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
School quality, wealth, test preparation, legacy preferences, recruitment, and evaluation discretion shape applicant pools.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Race-neutral socioeconomic measures may target disadvantage more precisely while avoiding racial classifications and new unfairness.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Evidence that neutral selection offers equal opportunity and comparable outcomes without inherited-advantage distortions would strengthen color-blindness.

**5. LANDING**  
Broaden merit and opportunity without converting demographics into automatic entitlements.

## Q11 — ELITE FPS / ESPORTS SEX GAP

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Existing evidence supports environmental barriers but does not establish that they fully cause the elite ceiling. A 2025 systematic review identified toxicity, discrimination, sexualization, stereotyping, and exclusion, while also acknowledging methodological limitations and a small evidence base. To distinguish environment from self-selection, studies need longitudinal cohorts tracking entry, practice, skill growth, retention, team access, and advancement under differing conditions. Current evidence does not justify treating psychomotor or interest explanations as proven, forbidden, or merely rhetorical.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Entry rates, harassment, visibility costs, practice opportunities, networking, team selection, and tournament economics may interact.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Large participation and practice-volume differences could generate extreme-tail representation even under unbiased selection.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Randomized or strong quasi-experimental interventions that remove barriers without changing retention or elite conversion would weaken the environmental account.

**5. LANDING**  
Interventions work when they improve safe participation, retention, skill development, and fair advancement.

## Q12 — IAT AND IMPLICIT BIAS AS POLICY

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The IAT is not strong enough to diagnose individual prejudice or justify adverse hiring decisions by itself. Meta-analysis finds small, heterogeneous relationships with intergroup behavior, with results varying substantially by methodology. Earlier work found modest average predictive validity, not deterministic prediction. Training and process audits may still be justified by broader evidence, but criticisms of reliability and construct validity are substantive scientific questions, not presumptively motivated skepticism.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Task order, familiarity, category salience, scoring, context, and imperfect attitude-behavior correspondence affect scores.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Even an imperfect measure can reveal aggregate associations that self-report misses in socially sensitive domains.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
High test-retest reliability, strong prospective prediction, intervention responsiveness, and low false-classification rates would support individual policy use.

**5. LANDING**  
Use the IAT as a limited research or educational aid, not a verdict on a person.

## Q13 — FAMILY STRUCTURE AND CHILD OUTCOMES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Family structure should not be moralized, but neither is it defensible to dismiss it as merely a proxy. The National Academies treats family structure as a possible driver while emphasizing interactions among demographics, labor markets, income, incarceration, and government policy. The actionable targets are economic security, stable caregiving, reduced conflict, childcare, housing, and supportive parental involvement, regardless of household label. Policies that enforce a marital norm can stigmatize families and overlook whether a particular relationship is safe or stable.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Income, caregiver time, instability, parental conflict, neighborhood conditions, and selection into family arrangements all matter.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Two stable caregivers can supply additional time, income, monitoring, and resilience, giving structure an independent causal role.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Strong designs showing that legal marital status itself dominates resources, stability, and relationship quality would challenge it.

**5. LANDING**  
Support stable, safe caregiving rather than prescribing one family form.

## Q14 — SEX PAY AND OCCUPATIONAL GAPS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The raw pay gap describes an outcome but does not isolate discrimination; the adjusted residual also depends on whether controls are confounders, mediators, or consequences of discrimination. Hours, occupation, career interruptions, employer practices, and parenthood should therefore be explained rather than mechanically controlled away. Longitudinal evidence finds that earnings gaps widen after family formation and that mothers often reduce hours or move toward less time-intensive work. Equality requires equal pay for comparable work, fair promotion and hiring, realistic caregiving support, and freedom from coercive stereotypes, not necessarily identical occupational distributions.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Care burdens, schedule premiums, bargaining, discrimination, occupational culture, and differing constraints jointly shape earnings.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Compensating differentials and voluntary preferences may explain substantial portions of observed sorting without employer discrimination.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Equalized caregiving, workplace flexibility, evaluation, and opportunity followed by unchanged gaps would strengthen preference-based accounts.

**5. LANDING**  
Study the full pathway while preserving both causal rigor and genuine choice.

## Q15 — USE OF FORCE AND RACIAL DISPARITY

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Population, encounter, and offending benchmarks answer different questions; none is universally correct. Population rates measure total civic burden, while encounter rates may illuminate officer decisions conditional on contact but can inherit bias in who is stopped. Offending benchmarks may be relevant for exposure to particular incidents, yet offending itself is measured imperfectly and does not justify unequal force against similarly situated people. A justice-first analysis should report several denominators, examine how encounters are generated, compare comparable circumstances, and assess necessity, proportionality, outcomes, and accountability.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Deployment, stop selection, civilian behavior, call patterns, officer discretion, neighborhood conditions, and data missingness affect estimates.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Ignoring encounter exposure can confuse differential contact rates with discriminatory decisions during otherwise comparable encounters.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Complete incident-level data showing equivalent encounter generation, treatment, force thresholds, and outcomes would weaken systemic-disparity interpretations.

**5. LANDING**  
Use multiple benchmarks without allowing any denominator to erase unequal exposure or treatment.
