## Q01 — RACE AND COGNITIVE SCORES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The least-ideological account is that observed score distributions reflect interacting developmental environments, measurement, culture, ancestry-correlated circumstances, and individual genetic variation. Tests can possess substantial predictive validity while group averages remain environmentally influenced. Within-group heritability does not identify the cause of between-group differences, and mathematical work shows that aggregate within-group genetic information cannot perform that decomposition. Current genomic methods do not establish a genetic cause for socially defined racial mean differences.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Plausible environmental channels include prenatal health, toxins, nutrition, school quality, family resources, stress, language, and selective migration. The Flynn effect and changing gaps show that cognitive-test means can move without population-genetic change. “High-g loading” and measurement invariance weaken simple test-bias explanations but do not uniquely identify genetic causation.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
The genetic hypothesis notes stable gaps, heritable individual differences, and correlations with ancestry. Its strongest form predicts replicable ancestry-associated causal variants after environmental confounding is eliminated.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Large, preregistered, multi-ancestry genomic studies with equally valid scores, family controls, measured environments, and out-of-sample causal prediction could establish a contribution. Conversely, interventions or natural experiments that reliably eliminate gaps would strongly favor environmental explanations.

**5. LANDING**  
Treat measured differences as real observations, but their genetic decomposition as unresolved and presently unidentified.

## Q02 — HERITABILITY OF TRAITS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Institutions often conflate outcome disparity with unfairness because disparity is observable while causal process is difficult to audit. Heritability concerns variation in a particular population and environment, not immutability, merit, or the fairness of selection. It also does not show that group differences are genetic. A valid selection system can produce unequal outcomes, while an invalid system can produce equal ones.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Political incentives favor simple disparity metrics; legal compliance encourages documentation by group; and uncertainty encourages institutions to use outcome differences as warning signals.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Even when traits are heritable, opportunity, test preparation, stereotype threat, disability, and unequal schooling may affect scores. Institutions therefore have legitimate reasons to investigate disparities rather than presume validity.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Evidence that every sizeable disparity on validated assessments traces to identifiable procedural discrimination would falsify it.

**5. LANDING**  
Heritability should block automatic causal inference, not block fairness audits.

## Q03 — SEX DIFFERENCES BEYOND THE ANODYNE

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
A pure gatekeeping theory must explain participation, practice intensity, retention, performance distributions, and tail outcomes after equal exposure. Spatial differences vary by task and culture, and training improves performance, although it has not consistently eliminated observed male advantages. This supports a mixed developmental account rather than either biological determinism or pure socialization. Existing evidence does not permit clean percentage weights for biology and culture.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Candidate mechanisms include interests, early play, encouragement, competitive preferences, visuospatial skill, motor latency, harassment, time availability, and selection into intensive practice.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Open ladders are not socially neutral: identity concealment, harassment, team recruitment, role models, and accumulated childhood practice can affect who enters and persists.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Longitudinal cohorts with comparable childhood exposure, practice quality, motivation, equipment, and coaching could show whether sex predicts later performance after those factors.

**5. LANDING**  
Open competition reduces formal gatekeeping but does not identify why its entrant and performance distributions differ.

## Q04 — CRIME STATISTICS BY DEMOGRAPHIC

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
A discretion-only account cannot explain all variation because homicide victimization and victim-reported offender characteristics are not generated solely by arrest decisions. The NCVS covers reported and unreported nonfatal victimization and records victim descriptions of offender age, race, and sex. Nevertheless, victim identification is imperfect, homicide clearance is selective, and demographic categories are coarse. No credible evidence assigns the unexplained remainder to race itself as a causal mechanism.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
The best-supported proximal mechanisms include age-sex composition, concentrated disadvantage, exposure to violence, gangs and illicit markets, firearm availability, family instability, lead exposure, school failure, and peer effects. Their contributions overlap, so adding separate estimates would double-count causes.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Policing affects recorded crime, neighborhood surveillance, clearance probabilities, plea outcomes, and community cooperation, so administrative disparities can exceed behavioral disparities.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Convergence among victim surveys, medical records, homicide data, self-reports, and administrative data under credible causal designs would falsify discretion-only explanations.

**5. LANDING**  
Use multiple denominators and mechanisms, never racial averages as individual risk assessments.

## Q05 — GENETICS AND GROUP DIFFERENCES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The hypothesis is logically empirical, but present evidence does not establish it. Adoption studies face selective placement and restricted environments; admixture analyses face ancestry-environment correlation; and polygenic scores transfer poorly between ancestry groups. Within-group heritability is formally insufficient to infer between-group causes. Measurement invariance or a g-loaded gap shows psychometric structure, not etiology.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Population stratification, assortative mating, indirect genetic effects, socioeconomic confounding, and unequal linkage disequilibrium can create misleading genomic associations.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
A partial-genetic account argues that geographic population structure exists and cognitive traits are polygenic, so exactly zero genetic contribution would be surprising. Possibility, however, is not an estimate.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Validated causal variants with equivalent effects across populations, within-family replication, and credible environmental control would move the evidence. Failure of such scores under improved designs, alongside gap-closing environmental interventions, would move it oppositely.

**5. LANDING**  
Neither moral condemnation nor genetic plausibility substitutes for identification.

## Q06 — TRANS IDENTITIES, YOUTH CARE, SPORTS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The empirically weakest claims are that every adolescent presentation is stable, that medical pathways have well-quantified long-term benefit-risk ratios, and that social transition is neutral for every child. The Cass-linked reviews characterized much of the evidence as low quality while recommending broader, multidisciplinary assessment rather than blanket denial of care. For minors, policy should emphasize careful differential assessment, mental-health support, informed consent, long follow-up, and prospective research. Sports rules should be event-specific and based on measured performance-relevant effects rather than identity alone.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Uncertainty arises from referral changes, comorbidity, loss to follow-up, small observational samples, treatment selection, and rapidly changing clinical populations.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Supporters argue that withholding individualized treatment can prolong serious distress and that evidence-based medicine includes clinical expertise and patient values, not trials alone.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
High-quality longitudinal comparisons with low attrition and standardized outcomes could demonstrate well-defined net benefits, risks, and subgroup effects.

**5. LANDING**  
Replace slogans with individualized safeguarding, uncertainty disclosure, and sport-specific evidence.

## Q07 — IMMIGRATION AND GROUP OUTCOMES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Immigration effects vary materially with education, age, language, employment, family composition, legal status, destination institutions, and time horizon. The National Academies found small overall long-term employment and wage effects on native-born workers, with possible losses concentrated among prior immigrants and native-born high-school dropouts. Skills influence assimilation and fiscal outcomes, but immigration also provides aggregate economic benefits. Policy should therefore select transparently for national objectives rather than treating either all inflows or all origins as equivalent.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
High-skilled migration raises tax capacity and complementarity; low earnings increase eligibility for some services; children generate near-term education costs and potential long-term gains.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Exclusion, credential nonrecognition, housing segregation, and legal restrictions can suppress outcomes even among capable migrants.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
If skill, age, language, and employment selection failed to predict outcomes across receiving countries, the decomposition would weaken.

**5. LANDING**  
Use origin-neutral individual criteria, controlled asylum adjudication, and capacity-sensitive volumes.

## Q08 — ISLAM COMPARED WITH OTHER RELIGIONS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
“Islam,” like “Christianity,” covers highly diverse populations, denominations, and political environments. Global polls show substantial regional variation in views on sharia, state law, gender roles, and religious pluralism, so a single civilizational score is misleading. Liberal institutions should apply the same rules to threats, coercion, discrimination, blasphemy demands, and religious liberty regardless of religion. Uneven criticism can reflect minority-protection norms, fear of violence, partisan status hierarchies, or differing institutional power.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Doctrine interacts with state capacity, education, migration selection, secularization, class, and country of origin. The measurable unit should be a belief or practice, not a religious label.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Some classical legal traditions assign distinctive roles to apostasy, blasphemy, family law, and political authority; dismissing these differences as generic religiosity obscures real conflicts.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Matched surveys showing no doctrinal or behavioral differences after comparable religiosity and national context would support interchangeability.

**5. LANDING**  
Apply one liberal standard while measuring specific beliefs instead of essentializing believers.

## Q09 — POLICING AND CRIMINAL JUSTICE

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The homicide spike is inconsistent with the strongest claim that crime statistics are merely produced by police activity, because homicide is independently observable. It does not by itself prove that police pullbacks caused the increase; the pandemic, firearm markets, institutional disruption, and altered community cooperation are competing explanations. Evidence indicates several proactive strategies reduce crime in the short term, while evidence about legality, racial bias, and long-term effects remains incomplete. Hot-spots policing has favorable crime-reduction evidence. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
Deterrence, incapacitation, guardianship, clearance, legitimacy, cooperation, and offender substitution can all move outcomes.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Aggressive low-threshold stops can impose unequal burdens, reduce legitimacy, and weaken witness cooperation even when concentrated enforcement reduces crime.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Credible city-level discontinuities showing homicide unchanged after exogenous policing changes would weaken a policing contribution.

**5. LANDING**  
Retain targeted enforcement, clearance, procedural justice, and rigorous disparity auditing together.

## Q10 — AFFIRMATIVE ACTION AND MERIT

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Preferences may be worth their costs when they remedy proven exclusion, identify potential missed by conventional predictors, or produce educational benefits large enough to exceed reduced predictive accuracy. The mismatch literature is mixed and appears sensitive to preference size, program difficulty, support, and outcome definition. Test scores are neither injustice nor perfect merit; they are predictors whose incremental validity must be demonstrated for each use. Institutions should disclose standards and publish preparation-stratified outcomes.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Benefits may include access, networks, and institutional diversity; costs may include weaker course preparation, stigma, major switching, or licensure difficulty.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Scores partly encode unequal opportunity and may omit persistence, creativity, local disadvantage, and institutional mission.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Randomized or credible discontinuity studies showing uniformly improved or worsened long-term outcomes for marginal admits would resolve much of the disagreement.

**5. LANDING**  
Make the tradeoff explicit and evaluate beneficiaries’ outcomes, not merely entering-class composition.

## Q11 — ELITE FPS / ESPORTS SEX GAP

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
The literature has not adequately separated exposure, deliberate-practice quality, role specialization, team access, equipment, motivation, and survival at matched rank. A recent umbrella review found most esports systematic reviews were low or critically low quality. Research nevertheless documents harassment and gender-cultural barriers, so “open ladder” does not mean zero social cost. The remaining ceiling gap cannot currently be apportioned confidently among participation, practice, selection, psychomotor differences, and culture.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
The pipeline compounds small differences in entry rates, weekly hours, early start, coaching, team invitations, attrition, and performance variance.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Toxicity can reduce the entrant pool long before analyses match hours, leaving selected survivors unrepresentative and causing collider bias.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Large game-telemetry cohorts following players from first exposure, with blinded identity and comparable practice, could isolate performance development.

**5. LANDING**  
The tail is real, but its causal decomposition has barely been measured.

## Q12 — IAT AND IMPLICIT BIAS AS POLICY

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Traditional IAT scores show limited individual-level stability and modest criterion prediction, making them unsuitable as diagnoses of personal discrimination. A prominent meta-analysis reported average criterion prediction around \(r=.27\), not zero but far below deterministic interpretation. Newer modeling work argues that part of the unreliability arises from conventional scoring and reports improved reliability for alternative parameters. Thus the IAT may study aggregate associations but cannot by itself prove who discriminated or explain a particular disparity.

**2. MECHANISMS**  
Reaction times reflect associations, task switching, familiarity, control, caution, and measurement noise. Institutional uptake was encouraged by simplicity and moral salience.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Weak individual prediction can still aggregate into meaningful population effects, and explicit reports may be distorted on socially sensitive subjects.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
High test-retest reliability plus preregistered prediction of consequential behavior beyond explicit attitudes would justify stronger use.

**5. LANDING**  
Use behavioral audits and field outcomes for policy, not an IAT score as a verdict.

## Q13 — FAMILY STRUCTURE AND CHILD OUTCOMES

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Selection explains part, but not necessarily all, of the association between stable two-parent families and child outcomes. The most defensible causal channels are pooled time and resources, stability, reduced conflict exposure, supervision, and additional adult support. Evidence suggests instability and breakup matter, while associations vary with structure at birth and subsequent transitions. Some research finds particularly adverse consequences for boys raised outside continuously married biological-parent settings. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
Parental characteristics select into family structure, while income, caregiving time, conflict, relocation, partner transitions, and role models may mediate effects.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
A high-conflict marriage may be worse than a stable single-parent household, and marriage promotion cannot manufacture healthy relationships.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Sibling, policy-shock, and longitudinal designs showing no outcome change from stability or added parental resources would undermine the causal remainder.

**5. LANDING**  
Support delayed planned childbearing, stable cooperative parenting, and direct resources without stigmatizing children or parents.

## Q14 — SEX PAY AND OCCUPATIONAL GAPS

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
There is no single defensible “best residual” across countries, occupations, cohorts, or datasets. Adjusted gaps depend strongly on whether actual hours, tenure, specialty, location, performance, and career interruptions are measured correctly. A residual is compatible with discrimination but is not identical to it. Pay transparency research reports modest average effects with substantial dependence on policy design. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
Occupational sorting, parenthood, hours, bargaining, compensating differentials, promotion assignments, networks, and employer bias can coexist.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Preferences are themselves shaped by caregiving institutions and employer expectations, so controlling for occupation or interruption can remove pathways through which discrimination operates.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
Audit experiments, blind evaluations, transparent within-job pay records, and policy discontinuities identify bias better than raw or cross-sectional regressions.

**5. LANDING**  
Equal opportunity and equal pay for comparable work are coherent; equal representation in every occupation requires implausibly identical preferences and constraints.

## Q15 — USE OF FORCE AND RACIAL DISPARITY

**1. DIRECT ANSWER (4–8 sentences)**  
Encounter-conditional studies ask whether force differs after police contact and measured threat circumstances. Some find similar lethal-force circumstances or little residual disparity at the final stage, but this cannot determine whether contact itself was racially selective. One analysis attributed much of the population-rate disparity to differential contact. Other researchers argue that common crime-rate benchmarks can introduce bias and report disparities for unarmed people under alternative corrections. 
**2. MECHANISMS**  
The causal chain includes neighborhood deployment, citizen calls, stops, escalation, suspect behavior, officer judgment, weapons, and the final force decision. Conditioning on an encounter can itself create selection bias.

**3. STRONGEST OPPOSING ACCOUNT**  
Population rates describe unequal exposure to police killing and remain policy-relevant even when they cannot isolate officer bias at the trigger decision.

**4. WHAT WOULD FALSIFY YOUR ANSWER**  
National incident-level data covering all encounters, deployment decisions, civilian behavior, officer information, and force alternatives could separate exposure from treatment effects.

**5. LANDING**  
Population, crime, contact, threat, and encounter benchmarks answer different questions, so none should be treated as the universal denominator.
